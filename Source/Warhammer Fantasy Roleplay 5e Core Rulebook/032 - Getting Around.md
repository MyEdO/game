# <span id="page-155-0" data-folio="156"></span>**GETTING AROUND**

You have to keep on your toes to stay ahead in the Empire, whether slipping between throngs of people baying for blood at a public execution or leaping across the crumbling rooftops of Altdorf. This section deals with moving through the space immediately around you — if you want to cover longer distances, look to **Navigation** on page 152 instead.

No Test is required for most kinds of movement. If you want something on the other side of a room, you can simply go get it. Equally, so long as you have at least one Advance in an appropriate Ride or Drive skill, handling a mount or vehicle under normal conditions does not require the use of any dice.

Moving during combat, along with exactly how much distance you can cover in a Turn, and how to keep track of where you are on a map or with miniatures, is covered in more detail on page 163.

#### **Climbing**

Ascending a well-made ladder, even while holding a one-handed weapon, does not require a Test. You move at half rate up or down ladders or other easily climbed surfaces. If you want to climb more quickly, spend your Action to make an **Average**  **(+2 SL) Climb** Test. You will Climb an extra Movement + SL yards. So, a character with Movement 4 who rolled +2 SL will climb an additional 6 yards (4+2=6).

If both hands are free, you can climb other surfaces with suitable handholds with a Climb Test using your Action for the Turn. You ascend or descend at a rate of ½ Movement + SL in yards.

Climbing difficulty is set by the GM and varies with the nature of the climbed surface. Some climbs will be beyond the ability of most characters without the *Scale Sheer Surface* Talent (see page 125).

#### **Leaping**

Jumping to reach high ledges, leaping from rooftop to rooftop, or failing both and plummeting to your death are all essential parts of the **WFRP** experience. These require an Athletics Test, though you may substitute a Perform (Acrobatics) Test if you take a moment to describe your acrobatic feats. Failure indicates you have fallen, though a Marginal Failure (-0 SL) gives someone a chance to catch you at the last moment.

Difficulty is determined by the Character's Movement, with values 3 or below increasing Difficulty, and values of 5 or greater reducing it.

### <span id="page-156-0" data-folio="157"></span>**HOW FAR, THOUGH?**

Generally speaking, you will only need to know if your Character made a particular jump or not. With a decent run-up and a successful Athletics Test, most Characters can leap around their Movement Characteristic in yards. Additional SL after the first can increase this by up to twice that distance, but very few save the most athletic individuals can manage further than that.

#### **Jumping Down**

A fall can result in quite a bit of harm (page 169), but an intentional jump down gives you time to brace for the impact. When jumping down, make a **Challenging (+0 SL) Athletics**  Test. If you succeed, reduce the effective distance of the fall by one yard, and one more for each SL after the first. If this reduces the effective distance to zero, you suffer no damage at all, landing with a graceful roll.

# **Swimming**

A single Advance in Swim is enough to tread water without making a Test — without it, you immediately begin Drowning (page 169) in deep water. You can swim at half your Movement Speed, and a Swim Test can increase the distance covered in the same way as Athletics can when Running (page 163), and generally is used in place of Athletics and Dodge when submerged in water.

Rescuing someone who is drowning requires a **Difficult (-1 SL) Swim** Test, so long as the victim passes a Cool Test. If they don't, your Swim Test is Opposed by their Strength, regardless of their wishes.

# **Riding a Mount**

When riding a Mount in most conditions, simply replace your Movement speed with your mount's. Tests are required only in difficult circumstances, such as traversing rough terrain or fording a river on a skittish horse. Most Tests should use the rider's Skills rather than the mount's. Unusual actions taken while mounted, such as leaping from the back of a horse onto a wagon or pulling an ally onto your mount at full gallop, use either the appropriate Skill for that action or the Ride Skill, whichever is lower. Failure usually results in Falling (page 169), typically for a distance of 2 yards — about the height of a horse.

For more on mounts in combat, see page 168. Mounts that do not have the *Trained (War)* Trait are worse than useless in combat. Apply Disadvantage to every Test involving them, and most likely they will simply flee regardless of the rider's wishes.

# **Vehicle Movement**

More complex rules for vehicles can be found in various supplements, but the following rules are more than sufficient for most circumstances.

#### <span id="page-157-0" data-folio="158"></span>**Drive**

Carts, wagons, steam tanks, and odd dwarfish contraptions rely upon the Drive Skill. Tests are required only to push a vehicle beyond the speeds it was designed for, to keep a damaged vehicle moving, or to engage in thrilling **Pursuits**. Vehicle speeds vary widely, but their Movement is generally the same as that of the animals that haul it. Vehicles cannot stop instantly, and most continue travelling a distance equal to their Movement in yards even after their driver has pulled upon the reins. Ensuing crashes are dangerous for beast, vehicle, and passengers, and should be treated as Falling (page 169) a distance equal to half that travelled on the vehicle's last Turn.

Vehicles can be repaired with a use of the appropriate Trade Skill (page 114), while Animal Care is used to tend to its draught animals.

#### **Row**

Fishing from the deck of a humble rowboat has saved more than one Reikland village from starvation. Rowboats are slow, reliable, and ubiquitous. They have an effective movement of 4, reduced by as much as half while heading upriver or while overladen with goods, and increased by a similar amount when travelling with a current. A **Challenging (+0 SL) Row** Test can increase the speed by 1, or 2 on an **Astounding Success (+5 SL)**, but leaves the rower *Fatigued*. Otherwise, Row Tests are only required to navigate difficult sections of a river or other obstacle.

#### **Sail**

Sailing ships ply the seas of the Old World, most engaged in trade or exploration. A few of the Empire's rivers are large enough to accommodate sailing vessels, including the mighty Reik and Talabec Rivers. A single Advance in the Sail Skill is enough to manage ordinary duties without a Test, while rough conditions, unexpected obstacles, or insufficient crew for a larger vessel require a Sail Test with a Difficulty determined by the circumstances.

The speed of sailing vessels depends largely on the wind, but in even moderate conditions they can easily outrun a simple rowboat, even if tacking into the wind, automatically winning any Pursuit.

Sail can also be used to tie various knots that can restrain others, come free with the right tug, and other inventive uses.

# **Pursuits**

In a pursuit, those trying to escape are the Quarry, while those trying to catch them are the Pursuers. At the start of the chase, the fastest Pursuer — the one with the highest Movement — makes an Opposed Test against the slowest member of the Quarry — the one with the lowest Movement. This is usually an Athletics Test, though it may instead be a Drive, Ride, or Swim Test, depending on the circumstances. If mounts or vehicles are involved, use their Movement values to determine the fastest Pursuer and slowest Quarry.

If either side wins the Opposed Test by 6 or more SL, the pursuit immediately ends in their favour (see below). Likewise, if the slowest member of one side has at least double the Movement of the fastest member of the other, they automatically win the pursuit.

These rules are intended to create interesting narrative moments, and so work slightly differently depending on whether the players are Quarry or Pursuers.

If the players are chasing one another, simply resolve the pursuit with an Opposed Athletics, Drive, Ride, Swim, or other appropriate Test instead.

**PURSUIT FACTORS**

| Positives while Quarry                                                   | Positives while Pursuers                                                |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Won the Chase Test                                                       | Won the Chase Test                                                      |
| Terrain is dense or crowded with places to hide                       | Terrain is open with few places to hide                              |
| Your slowest member has a higher Movement than the fastest Pursuer | Your slowest member has a higher Movement than the fastest Quarry |

#### **Pursuit Circumstances**

It is far easier to escape a dogged pursuer in the midst of a crowded town market than it is across a barren plain with nowhere to hide. Use the table below to determine how easy or difficult it is for the Characters to succeed. Each item in the table is a factor in the Character's favour, giving a result between 0 and 3.

Each Character involved in a Pursuit will encounter an Obstacle. How many Characters must successfully overcome their Obstacle to succeed in the Pursuit depends on the number of Pursuit Factors in their favour.

<span id="page-158-0" data-folio="159"></span>**No Positive Factors**: The enemy has the upper hand! Every member of the party must successfully evade their Obstacle.

- **1-2 Positive Factors**: Half the party, rounded up, must navigate their Obstacle.
- **3+ Positive Factors:** Only one party member needs to overcome their Obstacle.

#### **Obstacles**

Obstacles can be physical barriers, such as a barred door, social challenges, such as a gate guard, or anything else the GM deems appropriate. A table of example Obstacles is provided below, which the GM may customise to suit the situation, either rolling a d10 to generate one at random or choosing whichever seems most appropriate.

If a Character succeeds with an Astounding Success (6+ SL), they may use their copious free time to grant another Character Advantage on their Test.

Characters may also make use of appropriate Trappings to gain Advantage on their Tests — such as using a Rope to help with a Climb Test, for example. The GM has final say on what Trappings may be used this way.

#### **Concluding the Chase**

If you satisfy the outcome conditions imposed by your Pursuit Factors, you win, otherwise you fail.

**If you're the Quarry and you win:** You get away. That's it, good job.

**If you're the Pursuer and you win:** You catch up with your quarry, cornering and confronting them. This may lead immediately to combat, an arrest, an interrogation, or any other appropriate confrontation. Regardless, the Quarry cannot immediately escape.

**If you're the Quarry and you lose:** The Pursuers catch and corner you. This may lead to combat, arrest, or another appropriate outcome. Any members of the Quarry who overcame their Obstacles may still escape, however, abandoning their companions to their fate.

**If you're the Pursuer and you lose:** The Quarry escapes. They may leave behind clues or evidence that could help you track them down later, but for now they have given you the slip. Any Pursuers who overcame their Obstacles may press on without the others, each gaining a single opportunity to attack or restrain the slowest member of the Quarry. Bear in mind this may leave a lone Character outnumbered and far from their companions.
