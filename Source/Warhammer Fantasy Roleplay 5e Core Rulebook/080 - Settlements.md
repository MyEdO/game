# <span id="page-287-0" data-folio="288"></span>**SETTLEMENTS**

The Reikland is one of the most populated and civilised of the Empire's grand provinces. Nevertheless, great swathes of the realm are still wild, blanketed in forest and untouched by human hand. Even after 2,500 years of expansion, most Reikland settlements are found along the Reik and its many tributaries, reached by river if not always by road. Only in the southern breadbasket are rural settlements found spread across the landscape, connected by roads and canals.

# **Altdorf and the Manifold Towns**

It is said that all roads and rivers lead to Altdorf, the largest, richest city of the Empire, but there are many other interesting places to visit beyond that city's high, white walls. Numerous towns spread across the Reikland, including several freistadts, which strive to keep noble rule limited only to matters of war.

#### **Altdorf**

His Imperial Highness Emperor Karl Franz I of House Holswig-Schliestein, Elector Count, Grand Prince of the Reikland, Prince of Altdorf, Count of the West March, and Sigmar's Chosen, holds court in Altdorf, the capital of the Reikland and the whole Empire. It stands tall at the confluence of the mighty Reik and Talabec rivers, where many major trade routes converge. Little wonder it is the wealthiest city in the Empire. Every day, fresh, wide-eyed hopefuls arrive in the city, making for a cosmopolitan, rowdy populous.

Merchants, nobles, and diplomats from across the Old World and beyond mingle with folk of every rank, species, and creed. Elves, dwarfs, and even wizards are common sights, rarely attracting more than a wary glance or muttered prayer. Such a vast and varied population gives Altdorf an energy found nowhere else in the Empire, but also one of its most infamous characteristics: the Great Reek. During the height of summer, the stench of sewage and stagnant water grows so overpowering that many wealthy citizens retreat to their country estates.

Each morning, mist rolls in from the Flats to shroud the city before gradually lifting with the sun. When the fog lingers, tempers often fray. Altdorf 's citizens are easily whipped into a mob by the city's many agitators, and riots can erupt at the slightest provocation, real or imagined. Whatever the cause, the crown is always swift to crush unrest before it spreads.

# **ASUR IN ALTDORF**

There is a noticeable elf population in Altdorf — founded by high elf merchant princes trading up the Reik from Marienburg over a century ago. The asur were active in the city before this, as it was Teclis of Saphery who established the Colleges of Magic and there have been high elves present since that time. Saphery has established a permanent embassy and there are ambassadors from Ulthuan's other kingdoms — including the Chief Diplomat from Lothern, Daendra Stillwater, who is one of the emperor's privy councillors.

Asur in the Reikland gravitate to Altdorf, where they feel more comfortable around their peers. The streets of Turmgarten are asur merchants deal with the elf clans in Marienburg.

<span id="page-288-0" data-folio="289"></span>Altdorf is also a renowned centre of learning. The sons and daughters of the wealthy matriculate at the University of Altdorf, regardless of academic ability. The famed Imperial Engineers School, founded by Tilean genius Leonardo da Miragliano, also lies within Altdorf 's walls. The most mysterious places of learning in Altdorf are the Colleges of Magic. Founded over two centuries ago, they train magically-gifted citizens to channel their dangerous talents in the defence of the Empire.

Standing in stark contrast to the arcane mysteries of the Colleges of Magic, Altdorf is also the beating heart of the Cult of Sigmar. Sigmar's own city now boasts more Sigmarite temples and holy sites than all the other cities of the Empire combined, including the Grand Cathedral of Sigmar.

Another landmark is the Imperial Zoo, which boasts myriad monsters and animals, including the hulking Abomination of Stirland and the grotesque Drakwald Gibberbeast, each caged and displayed for the amusement of all.

#### **THE SKAVEN OF UNDER-ALTDORF**

Beneath the mud of the human city, there is a hive of verminous activity, where the ratmen scheme in sodden tunnels that worm through the ground. The inhabitants are relatively disciplined by skaven standards, thanks to the dominance of Clan Mors and their militaristic tendencies. The clans of Skryre, Eshin, and Scrutens connive against one another, however, for influence and warpstone.

Given its location beneath the Empire's capital, the skaven's Council of Thirteen are in turmoil about the fate of Under-Altdorf. Some see a marvellous opportunity to destroy the great city of men and leave the Empire vulnerable to the children of the Horned Rat. Other clan lords believe that Under-Altdorf should grow into a great skaven city, second only to Skavenblight in its eminence.

#### **THE IMPERIAL CALENDAR**

Two moons cross the night sky. Mannslieb, the larger, governs the tides and is sacred to Manann, completing its cycle every 25 days. The smaller Morrslieb is an ill-omened moon, believed to have been forged by Morr from the wreckage of a daemonic invasion, and few willingly travel beneath its light.

The Imperial year lasts 400 days, divided into twelve months of 32 or 33 days and six unnumbered festival days. Four mark the solstices and equinoxes, while the other two — Geheimnisnacht (Night of Mystery) and Hexensnacht (Witching Night) — occur when both moons are full and are widely feared.

The months are Nachhexen (After Witching), Jahrdrung (Year-Turn), Pflugzeit (Plough Tide), Sigmarzeit (Sigmar-Tide), Sommerzeit (Summertide), Vorgeheim (Before Mystery), Nachgeheim (After Mystery), Erntezeit (Harvest-Tide), Brauzeit (Brewmonth), Kaldezeit (Chillmonth), Ulriczeit (Ulric-Tide), Vorhexen (Before Witching).

There are eight days in the Imperial week. These are Wellentag (Workday), Aubentag (Levyday), Marktag (Marketday), Backertag (Bakeday), Bezahltag (Taxday), Konistag (Kingday), Angestag (Startweek), and Festag (Holiday). The origin of these names is lost to history, and now they carry little meaning. Markets are often open on days other than Marktags, and holidays vary according to the deities of local temples.

#### <span id="page-289-0" data-folio="290"></span>**Auerswald**

The lively free town of Auerswald stands by the confluences of the Teufel, Tranig, and Ober rivers, one of the busiest and best protected trade routes in the Reikland. The folk of Auerswald tend to be strong-willed and determined, but their easy charm and famous wit ensure that no matter how steely their negotiations, they rarely cause offence.

The bürgermeisters of the freistadt have taken to building vulgar townhouses to prove their success. Carved woodwork and elaborate gargoyles cover the buildings, drawing envious comments from rival guildsmen. Much of Auerswald is built on thick stilts over the floodplain of the river Teufel, which is often in spate. Connected by a bewildering labyrinth of ramps, bridges, and rope ladders, outsiders can easily become lost, especially when worse for wear.

Rumours abound that there is an organised gang of blackmailers and confidence tricksters operating from the town, though no witnesses have lived long enough to testify to their existence.

#### **Bögenhafen**

Bögenhafen is the largest, most prosperous town in the Vorbergland and serves as the crossroads of the region. It is a crucial stop on the principle trade route between the Empire and Bretonnia, lying equidistant between Altdorf and Montfort.

Bögenhafen is largely governed by its town council, with the local nobility content to let them run the place so long as the taxes keep flowing. Members of the Merchants Guild dominate the council, along with representatives from the Stevedores and Teamsters Guild.

#### **Diesdorf**

Diesdorf is a distinctive sight for travellers on the Reik, as almost every roof is thatched, testament to the town's reliance on the trade in wheat, most of which is shipped downriver to Altdorf. Visitors may conclude, however, that its chief export is religious fervour.

A little over 200 years ago, Magnus the Pious gave a legendarily powerful speech in the town. Since that day, Diesdorf has become a major pilgrimage site for devout Sigmarites. On holy days, the town's population doubles, with the numerous temples and shrines to Sigmar full of worshippers in prayer. Most families in the town have at least one member serving in the cult's priesthood.

#### **Dunkelberg**

The southern market town of Dunkelberg stretches across several hills along the banks of the River Grissen. The older, richer portions of the town sit atop the hills behind high stone walls, giving the wealthiest townsfolk a commanding view of the surrounding countryside and a sense of security. The poor districts are more vulnerable to goblin warbands, who occasionally strike out from their lairs in the Grey Mountains and the depths of the wild Graugrissen Forest.

Despite its size, Dunkelberg has a rustic air, with local farmers selling livestock and produce from markets lining the winding streets. Outside the town walls, on cloudless days, the Mercers Guild pay farmers to lay out linen between crops in so-called bleachfields, where the sun dyes the fabric white.

#### **Eilhart**

The vineyards around Eilhart produce succulent grapes, the source of a wine widely considered one of the Reikland's best whites. It is renowned for its crisp, light flavour, and the famously mild hangovers it leaves behind. The town also produces sharp, acidic beers which make use of fragrant hops and local grain.

Given the excellent quality of its alcoholic beverages, Eilhart has become a popular destination for epicures seeking to sample its wares from the many drinking houses, breweries, and vineyards.

#### <span id="page-290-0" data-folio="291"></span>**Grünburg**

Grünburg's boatwrights craft high quality barges, found plying their trade along the whole length of the Reik. The Boatwrights Guild is one of the most powerful on the town council, with many of the other guilds subservient to its dictates.

The open land to the southeast of Grünburg is known as the Battle Plains. Long ago, these verdant fields witnessed the Battle of Grünburg, where the fearsome orc warlord Gorbad Ironclaw was countered in a clash of mounted troops. The Battle Plains now attract interest from antiquarians and grave robbers, all keen to excavate debris from the blood-soaked earth.

#### **Holthusen**

Nestled along the River Schilder, Holthusen is a major point on the trade route between Eilhart and Marienburg. The coopers of Holthusen make particularly sturdy barrels, most famously the Holthusen Hogshead, which is so tough that one can be used as a butt for a whole day's worth of archery practice without spilling a single drop. Most vintners and brewers in the western Vorbergland use Holthusen barrels, and many age their stock in cool, subterranean warehouses beneath the town.

Holthusen nestles behind several rings of tall palisades, as wild beastmen from the nearby Reikwald regularly attack without warning. Folk believe the beasts have an insatiable thirst for alcohol, so the town has taken to leaving barrels of cheap wine at the forest's edge to placate the beastmen. Should any witch hunters hear of this appeasement, there may be a reckoning.

#### **Kemperbad**

An ancient town with a long and convoluted history, the Grand Freistadt of Kemperbad stands atop a 500-foot cliff above the Reik and River Stir. Though part of the Reikland, Kemperbad lies on the eastern bank of the Reik; historically the town has been fought over, and ruled by, nobles from Talabecland, Stirland, and the Reikland, changing hands time and again through a series of marriages, treaties, and wars. In 1066 IC it gained a charter from Emperor Boris, and since that day, Kemberbad has been a fiercely independent freistadt, celebrated for making the finest brandy in the Reikland.

Thanks to the town's prodigious wealth, the wealthy of Kemperbad are legendarily ostentatious, dressing in outfits so gaudy as to shame a peacock. Unsurprisingly, the neighbouring nobility regard Kemperbad's swollen coffers with envious eyes, and would dearly like to destabilise the town.

#### **Schädelheim**

Schädelheim sits beside the Grootscher Marsh, where the River Mos meets the Reik. Its position on the busy trade route between Marienburg and Altdorf has made it prosperous, and the town boasts a large number of inns and berths for passing ships and barges. Ferries here regularly run travellers back and forth over both rivers, and it is a hub for the disparate communities of the nearby marshlands.

Just south of town stands an ancient temple dedicated to Morr. The grand, crumbling building has presided over Schädelheim since before its founding, surrounded by a vast garden of black roses and tombstones. Many of the locals have an especially strong affinity with Morr, which does not sit easily with some Reiklanders, who believe Sigmar should be paramount.

#### **Schilderheim**

Standing resolute at the confluence of the Schilder and Reik rivers, Schilderheim is one of the Reikland's most important trading towns. It is also home to a surprisingly diverse array of wildfowl, particularly wading birds. Most notable is the red crane, a sedentary river bird found predominantly on the Reik, known for using heavy stones to crack open freshwater clams.

The Stevedores Guild has recently raised its prices in response to the volume of trade passing through the town. To avoid extra costs, several merchants have taken to conducting their business on the river itself, swapping barges to avoid loading and unloading. Some of these merchant houses have found their wharves set alight, warehouses raided, and barges sunk. The stevedores deny everything, so the Merchants Guild will pay handsomely for proof they are responsible.

#### **Stimmigen**

Stimmigen is a busy market town in the Suden Vorbergland, with a bridge over the River Ober on the route to Ubersreik. Its lush orchards produce the Reikland's best known apple, the sweet, yet tart, Ernwald. The Ernwald only flourishes hereabouts and its cultivation has attracted a significant halfling population, whose delicious apple pies, pastries, and crumbles, not to mention crisp cider, can be found in local hostelries all year round, and especially during Pie Week at the start of autumn. All the folk of Stimmigen celebrate this halfling festival, as an opportunity for rich and poor alike to enjoy tastings and eating competitions, and generally gorge themselves on all manner of sweet, succulent, baked delicacies.

#### <span id="page-291-0" data-folio="292"></span>**Ubersreik**

The town of Ubersreik stands near Grey Lady Pass, one of only two major passes over the Grey Mountains into Bretonnia. Because of this, the town regularly sees traders and travellers from across the Old World. It has recently seen upheaval as the ruling von Jungfreuds were declared traitor by Emperor Karl Franz, and abandoned the town to seclude themselves at their ancestral seat in the Duchy of Black Rock to the west.

Ubersreik has a long association with the nearby dwarf clans of the Grey Mountains. Unusually, there are members of the city's dwarf population who sit upon the town council. The elder folk designed and built one of Ubersreik's more famous edifices, its spectacular bridge over the River Teufel. Widely regarded as one of the most impressive feats of engineering to be found in the Reikland outside of Altdorf, it connects the trade road which runs from Bögenhafen to Dunkelberg and then on to Nuln. Ubersreik deals in trade of all kinds, but is most well-known for ore and the skilled metalwork of the dwarfs.

#### **IMPERIAL DWARFS IN THE REIKLAND**

The old alliance between the Empire and dwarfs has existed since Sigmar's time and nowhere is it stronger than the Reikland. There have been times of persecution, but thanks to the preeminence of Sigmar's cult, many dwarf clans have settled alongside humans in towns along the Reik and in the hills. These tight-knit communities usually take traditional dwarfen trades in fine craft and construction, although some adopt more human roles (and vices) which their hidebound mountain kin would reject.

Highland and mining towns such as Hahnbrandt and Delfgruber typically have larger dwarf populations than agricultural settlements in the Vorbergland. The major cities and towns such as Altdorf, Dunkelburg, and Schilderheim have dwarf quarters where the resident clans hold sway.

#### **Weissbruck**

In the past century, Weissbruck has grown rapidly into a bustling port for goods and travellers moving between Bögenhafen and Altdorf. Several generations ago, the ruling Gruber family made a shrewd decision to mine the rich deposits of coal and iron found in the Skaag Hills. The revenue enabled them to commission dwarf engineers to construct a canal in 2462 IC, which brought wealth and new folk to the town.

Weissbruck retains the character of a typical mining town, yet the mines' output has diminished. More and more miners have descended from the Skaag Hills — a growing, agitated, unemployed workforce who have little to do but drink cheap rotgut and pick fights with strangers.

#### **Wheburg**

Wheburg is the first town encountered after leaving the Grey Mountains on the road from Helmgart to Bögenhafen. Mountain patrols pass through regularly, and troops are always billeted in the town's sizeable barracks. They have taken to displaying their banners in the streets, and walking through Wheburg, one can see the colours of numerous Reikland regiments.

The town is a welcome respite for travellers who have survived the mountains' extreme weather and the threat of orcs, goblins, or other monsters. Wheburg has a reputation for hospitality, and its locals are regarded as the friendliest in the Reikland, for a price. A visitor can buy almost any pleasure in the rowdy and licentious taverns, inns, and gambling dens lining the streets. Spending time in Wheburg prior to marriage has become something of a rite of passage for well-heeled Reiklanders.

# **The Bastions and Fortresses**

Many strongholds defend the Reikland, built to protect the province from invasion and its nobles from plague and disquiet amongst the commoners. In the south, bastions in the Grey Mountains defend against raiding goblins, trolls, and the restless dead, not to mention the neighbouring kingdom of Bretonnia. Along the Reik, castles from the Age of Three Emperors once defended the borders from other grand provinces. Today, many are little more than relics, their crumbling stonework lost to time. Others remain, a constant reminder to ambitious nobles in Middenland, Talabecland, Stirland, and Wissenland that the Reikland stands resolute.

#### **Blackstone Tower**

The Crooked Corridor is a narrow gorge, which winds through the Grey Mountains near the Wasteland border. It perches precariously on the edge of steep cliffs, far too tight for wagons or horses; the slightest slip of a foot could lead to a painfully inglorious end. It is completely impassable in the winter.

The Crooked Corridor was once known only to goat herders and smugglers, until a spate of goblin raids in 2454 IC brought the pass to the attention of the Emperor Mattheus II. He decreed the construction of a fortress here to watch for invasion, and so his masons raised Blackstone Tower from the local dark stone and slate to stand sentinel over the gorge.

<span id="page-292-0" data-folio="293"></span>Due to an oversight by surveyors, the new tower stood on land belonging to the dwarfs of Karak Ziflin. Not wanting to upset them, the emperor ceded the bastion, and today, the dwarfs repay the emperor's generosity by permitting a limited Reikland garrison of halberdiers and handgunners to barrack alongside their own troops at Blackstone Tower.

This is not a happy alliance. The tower is a powder keg of grievances and resentment, with the Reiklanders chafing under the dwarfs' leadership, and the dwarfs constantly belittling the shoddy human stonework they are working to replace.

#### **Steirlich Manor**

On the southern slopes of the Hägercrybs, in the Duchy of Grauwerk, sits a heavily fortified manor atop a windswept ridge. The manor is an ancient holding, built in the time of Emperor Mandred Ratslayer, who gifted tenure of the surrounding land to the von Bruner family in 1138 IC.

Graf Steirlich, who rules from the manor, is steely-eyed and ambitious, with a ruthless streak as wide as the Reik. He is determined to root out bandits and other menaces that dare impinge upon his property. Indeed, there are whisperings that he may be overly enthusiastic in his desire to investigate unnatural occurrences, for dark rumours of corruption swirl around the scions of the von Bruner line.

Nevertheless, the darkly handsome graf is always on the lookout for folk possessed of stout hearts and strong sword arms to help him maintain the peace and conduct his investigations.

#### **Helmgart**

The fortress town of Helmgart guards the border with Bretonnia from the eastern side of Axe Bite Pass. The Empire's relations with Bretonnia have not always been civil, and while the ramparts of Helmgart are no longer lined with aging skulls in elaborate helms, they most likely remain in the castle's cellars. These days, the soldiers of Helmgart patrol the pass, protecting travellers and merchants from goblins, bandits, and other menaces. Amongst the troops garrisoning the fortress are a number of storied regiments.

Long ago, the dwarfs carved Helmgart's keep directly from the granite mountainside. Three tiers of stout stone walls buttress the fortress town, offering an unparalleled view of the road below. Adjoining Helmgart proper is a huge, imposing wall broken only by a single long tunnel between the sheer mountainsides — the only route through to Bretonnia.

The fortress sits at one end of the great road that leads directly to Bögenhafen, and from there onto Altdorf. Helmgart is equal parts imposing citadel and bustling trade town. Here, Bretonnian merchants barter brandy, wine, fine fabrics, arms, and armour, while local dwarf clans trade ingots of silver, lead, and iron, as well as their own precious metalwork. With so many merchants and traders, the marktplatz of Helmgart is always abuzz, rife with endless opportunities and uncounted thieves.

# **The Villages, Hamlets, and Holy Places**

Most of the Reikland's villages and hamlets cluster close to towns for protection and trade. In the Vorbergland, settlements are strewn more freely amongst the farmlands, dotting the landscape every few miles or so, relying on fortress garrisons and regiments from the towns for protection.

The Reikland is home to an uncounted number of isolated monasteries, abbeys, way-temples, and other holy sites. Many stand in the most unlikely places, whether for religious or historical reasons, from mountainsides and the depths of forests to islands in the middle of lonely lakes.

#### **Monastery of the Holy Word**

Deep in the Reikwald west of Altdorf, far from any easily traversed road, stands the Monastery of the Holy Word. This hallowed site is tended by the Sigmarite Order of the Anvil, the monks responsible for preserving the religious laws and practices of the Cult of Sigmar.

The Monastery of the Holy Word houses the order's greatest treasure: the *Testaments of Sigmar*. After he departed the Empire, Sigmar's retainers recorded the accounts of those who knew him as a man. Historians and priests consider the *Testaments* the most accurate record of Sigmar's words and deeds, so it is little wonder that they are some of the holiest texts in the Empire.

The Monastery of the Holy Word will admit penitents and scholars who seek obscure details about the foundations of Sigmar's law. Many travel from across the Empire to query the black-and-green robed monks on doctrine or as part of a pilgrimage. None outside the order may consult, or even touch, the original documents, but they may gaze upon a few of the original pages if they can prove indisputable piety.

#### <span id="page-293-0" data-folio="294"></span>**Rottfurt**

Scholars and wizards throughout the Empire speak of Rottfurt in hushed, reverential tones. Though primarily a village of shepherds, trading wool and mutton, Rottfurt also produces a famous, and famously pricey, sheepskin parchment, Rottfurt Silver. The parchment possesses a faint sheen, takes ink well, and resists fading far longer than other, lesser parchments.

The thick-wooled, pale sheep of Rottfurt are the village's pride and livelihood. The shepherds indulge them, letting them feed on the lush grass of the Hammastrat Heights and wander freely during the day. The shepherds are both herders and militia, tasked with protecting their precious flocks at all costs.

#### **SLEEPING SHEPHERDS**

Of late, the sheep have been going missing. Despite their vigilance, those on guard have found themselves falling asleep. On waking, another sheep has vanished. The locals are angry and distraught, with many wild and spectacular theories on the cause of this woolly calamity.

The culprit is a local witch, Traudl Fluchschmied, who employs magic in order to lull shepherds to sleep while she steals their sheep. She is using the animals in an attempt to win the trust of a monster whose lair she has discovered in the nearby woods. If she is apprehended, she may be able to call this beast to defend her.

#### **Wörlin**

The fishing hamlet of Wörlin is almost impossible to spot from the river. The settlement is surrounded by a number of small, rocky islets and promontories, and sheltered by thousands of bowing trees. These Willows of Wörlin line the banks of the Reik for many miles around, and it seems none have any interest in using their lumber. A great deal of trade passes by Wörlin, but very little actually happens at its wharf, as most passing goods are bound for more lucrative markets.

#### **THE ROOT OF THE PROBLEM**

On the festival day of Sonnstill, the council of the Wörlin's elders gather to 'Water the Willows'. This simple ceremony involves singing, dancing, feasting, and the slicing of a virile stranger's throat, splashing blood across the thirsty roots of the Queen Willow, quenching her sanguinary appetites for one more year.

Once sated, the Queen is pleased, and will ensure the hamlet's safety from the children of the forest. Should anything interrupt the ceremony, the Queen awakens and summons her children, bringing a horde of braying beastmen to slaughter all in their path.

#### **Zahnstadt**

Isolated Zahnstadt is considered by locals to be the last village of the Vorbergland, after which the rolling hills turn barren and cold before reaching upwards to the Grey Mountains. In the shadow of those peaks, Zahnstadt skulks along the southern bank of the River Mos, and is nestled deep in a dark valley with bleak crags rising to all sides.

Even during the long days of summer, the sun only shines on the village briefly, hiding behind the surrounding cliffs in the morning, and creeping behind the mountains as the day lengthens. In the winter, there is barely any direct sunlight at all, locking the village in darkness. Despite this, the folk of Zahnstadt are welcoming. Its homely inn, the *Wayward Sun*, is famous for its ever-burning hearthfire and the upbeat songs that echo into the night.

# **THE SINS OF THE PAST**

Zahnstadt is in the grip of something far worse than a lack of sunlight: a vampire. As Mannfred von Carstein faced defeat in the Third Vampire War, Janos von Carstein deserted his lord and evaded pursuit by his vengeful minions. He eventually chanced upon dark Zahnstadt and saw it as the perfect place to go to ground.

Almost three hundred years later, he hides there still, and time has made him bold. He now sleeps beneath the *Wayward Sun* in an ostentatious, velvet-lined coffin, rising each evening to drink the blood of its visitors.

Rumours of Janos von Carstein have reached the witch hunters *and* the pawns of the recently resurrected Mannfred. It would be unfortunate if poor, unsuspecting souls happened to hostel in the *Wayward Sun* on the same night the various parties arrive to investigate...

# <span id="page-294-0" data-folio="295"></span>**The Ancient Sites and Terrible Ruins**

Prehistoric monuments and relics of ancient religion litter the Reikland, places of mystery and the forgotten folk who raised them. Most towns and villages boast their own local ruins, with accompanying eerie tales of haunting and darkness.

#### **The Darkstone Ring**

The path to the Darkstone Ring lies north of Blutroch, near the Altdorf-Bögenhafen road. It leads through woodland to a place of terrible, ancient power. When night falls, evil emanates from the old glade; the six suggestively-carved stones within glow green with a wan luminosity, flaring bright as Morrslieb waxes.

At the centre of the Darkstone Ring stands a monolithic slab of unidentifiable rock, permanently stained with the blood of the countless innocents sacrificed there throughout the millennia. Despite the ring's fell reputation, travellers are lured by legends of mystical potential and lost artefacts of terrible arcane power. Not even frequent sightings of beastmen and cultists, especially around the fated night of Geheimnisnacht, can deter these brave — some may say foolhardy — souls.

#### **Helspire**

Looming perilously in the peaks overlooking Axe Bite Pass, the Helspire is a grim ruin, the shell of an ancient fortress, its stones inlaid with the relics of countless fallen. No one knows who may have built the tower, and it is so remote that no Bretonnian duke or imperial baron would think it worthwhile to repair and garrison the place.

The villagers who live in the lee of the mountains tell strange tales about the citadel. They claim that on rare nights of occult significance, eldritch lights blaze upon its battlements, casting eerie shadows across the pass. At such times, so they say, the Helspire reveals itself as a citadel of unquiet dead, and skeletal knights sally forth to scour the mountainside, terrorising both Reiklander and Bretonnian alike.

Treasure hunters sometimes clamber up to the Helspire, daring to confront whatever necromantic horror lurks within its walls. Invariably, they return disappointed, having found nothing within the ruins but dust and old, yellowed bones.
