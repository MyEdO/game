# <span id="page-145-0" data-folio="146"></span>**NOSING AROUND**

The Empire is rife with secretive schemes and dark conspiracies. Nobles expend a hundred lives to move a border two fields over, while the servants of the Dark Gods work to undermine civilisation itself.

Characters will inevitably find themselves uncovering these plots — examining the site of a grisly murder, poring over dusty records, following whispered rumours, and prising secrets from those who would rather keep silent.

#### **HIDING CLUES**

Don't hide vital clues behind a Test. It can be tempting to make Players work for information, but if finding that information is essential to the adventure, they should find it.

Instead, allow Characters to notice one or two important clues straight away. Use Perception or Intuition Tests to reveal additional clues, useful insights, or a clearer understanding of what has already been discovered.

Let the Players piece the evidence together where possible, and if they still become stuck, provide another lead. If all else fails, a helpful NPC can always point the way. An unsolved mystery is intriguing — one the Characters never discover is simply frustrating.

**COMMON MEANS OF INVESTIGATION AND RESEARCH**

| Action | Test |
|---|---|
| Tell how an animal died | An Average (+2 SL) Animal Care Test, or Challenging (+0 SL) if a subtle poison or illness was the cause. |
| Tell a lie, or spot one | The liar makes a Charm Test, opposed by the target's Intuition. The Difficulty can increase if the lie is particularly outrageous. |
| Ask around about someone | A Gossip Test to learn what others think of the subject, with better rolls revealing more secretive or pertinent information. A successful Bribery Test can tell you someone is corrupt enough to take a bribe, and what their price might be. |
| Wring a secret from someone | A Charm Test, opposed by the target's Cool to trick them into letting something slip, or an Intimidate Test opposed by Cool to force the information from them. The Difficulty is set by just how sensitive the information is. |
| Search a room or a crime scene | A successful Perception Test spots hidden or subtle details, with better results revealing more information. A disturbed scene increases the Test's Difficulty. |
| Extract information from books | A successful Research Test will tell you what library or book to consult to learn something, or help you find pertinent facts buried within large and mouldering tomes. |
| Recall a useful fact | Having an Advance in a Lore Skill will tell you basic information about that subject, with a Test required only to recall particularly obscure or unusual information. |
| Spot a forgery | An Evaluate Test, with a Difficulty set by the quality of the work. |
| Follow someone's trail | A Track Test, with a Difficulty set by the conditions and age of the trail. Opposed by your quarry's Track only if they took the time to cover their trail. |

# <span id="page-147-0" data-folio="148"></span>**What You Already Know**

Even the most ignorant peasant in the Empire knows something of the world around them, even if it is only which cow provides the most milk and how much coin it will take to ensure their lord does not evict them come winter. The information in **Chapter 10** is common knowledge, and does not require a Test to know or remember.

**COMMON LORE SPECIALISATIONS**

| Specialisation | Information Provided                                                                                                                                                         |
|----------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Engineering    | Understand the application of levers, pulleys, inclined planes, and other simple machinery. Recognise the work of a specific artisan, or the principles underlying a complex machine.           |
| Geology        | Recognise specific types of stone, and determine where digging a well or mine is likely to prove fruitful, or judge if a cave is prone to flooding or likely to collapse.                       |
| Heraldry       | Recognise the banners and iconography of great noble houses and military outfits. Determine the meaning of the animals, colours, and other symbols used in a coat of arms.                      |

| Law            | Know what the law is and how it might be broken or circumvented, and make a compelling legal argument or recognise the flaw in one.                                                                |
| Magic          | Recall the names of the Winds of Magic and their effects, recognise the trappings of a particular magical tradition, or determine if a grimoire contains true spells or the ravings of the mad. |
| Metallurgy     | Determine what metals or alloys a weapon or tool is made of, how it was likely to have been forged, and how it might be repaired.                                                                  |
| Science        | Knowledge of natural philosophy — how the mundane phenomena appear to work, and the theory behind creating unusual compounds such as black powder or soap.                                      |
| Theology       | The practices and beliefs behind religions common to your culture, such as Sigmar, Taal, or Ulric, worshipped in the Empire, or Asuryan and Khaine, who are revered by the elves. |

#### **Lore**

A Character's Lore Specialisations represent their knowledge. If you have at least one Advance in an appropriate Lore, you automatically know basic facts about that subject. For example, a Character with Lore (Reikland) simply knows that the Reikland is a province of the Empire. Make a Lore Test only to recall unusual, obscure, or hidden information.

The more specific a Lore skill is to the question at hand, the more nuanced and detailed the information learned. For example, an **Average (+2 SL) Lore (Empire)** Test is sufficient to learn exactly where Helmgart is, while an **Average (+2 SL) Lore (Helmgart) Test** would reveal where it is, who rules it, the names of some prominent individuals there, and so on.

Lore represents theoretical knowledge about a subject, while Trade represents putting that knowledge into practice.

#### **Language**

Every Character speaks their native language fluently and does not need a Language Skill to use it. A Language Test is required only to convey especially complex ideas, understand obscure vocabulary or dialects, or pass as a native speaker. Once you have 4 Advances (+20) in a Language Specialisation, you speak that language fluently and no longer need to make Language Tests in most cases.

#### **Language Specialisations**

Some languages belong to the same family. Native speakers of one can usually hold a basic conversation with speakers of a related language, even without the appropriate Language Skill. They cannot read or write it, pass as a native speaker, or reliably understand complex ideas or unfamiliar vocabulary until they learn the Skill.

### **BATTLE TONGUE**

Battle Tongue is a specialised language that conveys complex tactical information in just a few words. A Character who passes a **Challenging (+0 SL) Language (Battle)** Test can use their Action to communicate up to 40 words of orders or tactical advice to others who know Language (Battle).

If players spend too long discussing tactics during combat, the GM can remind them that only Characters with Battle Tongue can exchange detailed plans in the heat of battle.

# <span id="page-149-0" data-folio="150"></span>**Research**

Most people in the Empire cannot read or write, so knowledge is usually spread by rumour, gossip, and proclamation. For the literate, however, books, records, and grimoires hold a wealth of information.

A Character with at least one Advance in Research can, given enough time, find straightforward information in a book or well-organised library without making a Test. Make a Research Test only when the information is obscure, or the source is poorly organised, incomplete, or difficult to understand.

If you do not even know where to begin, a **Challenging (+0 SL) Research Test** points you towards a useful source.

# **Asking Around**

People in the Empire are often wary of strangers, but everyone enjoys a good story. News of distant places, a round of drinks, or a few well-placed coins can loosen even the tightest lips.

#### **Good Roleplay**

Good and entertaining roleplay should be rewarded with useful information whenever appropriate, without requiring a Test. If the information is especially valuable or dangerous, a **Challenging (+0 SL) Charm Test** or a suitable bribe may be needed instead.

Players who prefer to rely on their Character's abilities rather than acting in character should still explain what they are trying to say or ask before making a Test. The GM can then decide whether a Test is required, and if so, which Skill and Difficulty are appropriate..

#### **Gossip**

A successful Gossip Test uncovers local rumours. The Difficulty depends on how willing people are to talk and where you find them. Swapping stories in a tavern might be Easy (+4 SL) or Average (+2 SL), while chatting with the guards outside your cell could be Difficult (-1 SL) or harder. Spreading a rumour is usually at least Difficult (-1 SL), depending on its target and credibility.

Rumours are not always true, but they should always be useful. Every vicious rumour about a hated noble is unlikely to be true, but the existence of such tales reveals that he is indeed hated.

#### **EXAMPLE OUTCOMES**

**An Average (+2 SL) Gossip Test to gather rumours about a nearby mine**

**Astounding Success (+5 SL):** All of the below, along with tales of strange folk descending into the mine each Gheimisnacht for reasons better left unlearned.

**Impressive Success (+3 to +4 SL):** You learn the below, plus the name of one of the former miners who still lives nearby.

**Success (+1 to +2 SL):** You learn that the mine was closed after a series of unusual deaths that saw labourers refuse to enter it.

**Marginal Success (+0 SL):** You learn that the mine closed a decade ago, but not why.

**Marginal Failure (-0 SL):** You learn nothing about the mine, but do notice that people seem reticent to talk about it, as though it is ill-omened in some way.

**Failure (-1 to -2 SL):** You learn nothing of use.

**Impressive Failure (-3 to -4 SL):** Rumours begin to circulate that you're a wealthy merchant interested in local mines, leading to several distracting and unhelpful inquiries from local labourers and land owners.

**Astounding Failure (-5 SL):** You waste the night drinking with a man who claims to know everything there is to know about the mine, only to learn that he was talking about a quarry three days' walk towards Altdorf.

#### **Interrogation**

To extract information from a specific individual, make a Charm or Intimidate Test opposed by the target's Cool. On a success, the target reveals something useful, whether by answering truthfully, telling an obvious lie, or giving themselves away through a notable omission.

# **Investigating**

Investigation is about interpreting clues, not finding them. Essential clues should never depend on a successful Test (see **Hiding Clues**, page 146), though careful investigation can uncover additional evidence and valuable insights.

#### **Searching a Room**

Searching a room usually requires a **Challenging (+0 SL) Perception Test**. Higher SL reveal better-hidden clues or provide additional insights. If several Characters search the same area, try to reward every successful Test with something useful, rather than letting the highest result reveal everything.

# <span id="page-150-0" data-folio="151"></span>**EXAMPLE OUTCOMES**

**A Challenging (+0 SL) Perception Test to search the scene of a murder**

**Astounding Success (+5 SL):** All of the below, and you spot flecks of unusual purple paint or dye beneath the fingernails — a rare substance available in very few places.

**Impressive Success (+3 to +4 SL):** As below, but you also notice two indents on the victim's nose — a sign that they wore spectacles, and were likely a learned man of some kind.

**Success (+1 to +2 SL):** As below, and you additionally notice that there is not enough blood to account for how deep the wounds are — he was almost certainly killed elsewhere, then moved here.

**Marginal Success (+0 SL):** You spot the body and notice that it appears to have been moved.

**Marginal Failure (-0 SL):** You spot the dead body and notice there is something odd in how it lies, but can't say how.

**Failure (-1 to -2 SL):** You notice the body, but learn nothing of use.

**Impressive Failure (-3 to -4 SL):** You notice the dead body, but become convinced that some irrelevant detail, such as a discarded cup, may be important.

**Astounding Failure (-5 SL):** You notice the dead body, but manage to leave bloody footprints throughout the room, muddying the scene and leaving evidence of your own presence in the process.

#### **Intuition and Insights**

An Intuition Test can be used to detect a lie (page 145) or gain useful insights about another person, such as their Status (page 140), profession, origins, or general mood. An appropriate Lore or Trade Skill may be used instead, and may even grant an easier Difficulty. For example, an **Easy (+4 SL) Trade (Cook) Test** is enough to realise that a shifty-looking chef has never held a filleting knife before.

Intuition Tests can also be used to connect clues and draw conclusions from information the Characters have already uncovered. If the Players reach the correct conclusion themselves, the GM should let them know that their conjecture makes sense without requiring a Test.

## **Tracking**

Use Track to follow a trail across the wilderness. If the trail is obvious — such as a bleeding victim or footprints in fresh snow — no Test is required.

Tracking over long distances is an Extended Track Test, with one Test every few hours or once per day, depending on the pace of the pursuit. If the quarry is actively fleeing, the GM may instead use the Pursuit rules (page 158).
