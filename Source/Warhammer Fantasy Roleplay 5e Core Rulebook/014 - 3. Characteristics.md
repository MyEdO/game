# <span id="page-37-0" data-folio="38"></span>**3. CHARACTERISTICS**

**A)** Roll **2d10** for each of the ten Characteristics in order and make a note of the results. If you are happy with these results, add each value to the appropriate Species modifier from the Characteristic Table, and record the totals on your Character Sheet.

#### **COMPARING CHARACTERISTICS**

A Characteristic score of 30 is the Human average, with a score of 40 indicating a gifted individual, and a sore of 60 or more being truly exceptional. Non-human Species have different ranges of ability — the average Elf is far more graceful than the most humans, and Dwarfs are far more strong willed.

If you stop here, note the three marked Characteristics from your first Career Level. Increase the starting level of these Characteristics by up to six in total — so increasing one by six, all three by two each, or any combination you wish.

If you're not content and want to swap some numbers around, go to step B. If you want to roll again, go to step C.

**B)** Rearrange the results from step A and assign them to Characteristics of your choice, then add the appropriate Species modifiers and record the totals on your Character Sheet. You may then increase the three Characteristics from your first Career Level by up to three in total, as above.

**C)** Roll ten new Characteristic scores and assign them as in step B, or ignore the dice and distribute **100 points** between the ten Characteristics (minimum 4, maximum 16 in each). Add the appropriate Species modifiers and record the totals on your Character Sheet. You gain no other increases to your Characteristics during character creation.

A summary of each Characteristic and what it does can be found on page 24. For a more detailed explanation, see **Chapter 4: Skills and Talents**. If you want to choose quickly, assign your highest values to the three Characteristics you made a note of from your Career. They are likely to be the most important to your Character's abilities.

You may increase your Characteristics during play by spending XP to purchase Characteristic Advances (page 191). **Each Advance increases the associated Characteristic by +5**.

# **Characteristic Bonuses**

The first or 'tens' digit of each Characteristic is its bonus value. Characteristic Bonuses are used in a variety of different ways throughout the rules.

**Example:** *Strength (S) 39 equates to a Strength Bonus of 3. Willpower (WP) of 51 equals a Willpower Bonus of 5.*
