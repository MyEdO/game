# <span id="page-193-0" data-folio="194"></span>**CHARACTER EVENTS**

Each player rolls d100 on the Character Events table to determine what's been happening to their Character during the Downtime. Some Events may impact Endeavours and other Downtime activities, while others have effects that linger for your next adventure.

#### **01–04: Inspiration Strikes**

Through diligent study or a flash of sudden inspiration, you make a breakthrough at improving your skills. Gain 50 XP.

#### **05–09: Unexpected Esteem**

Someone you helped in the past has a chance to repay your efforts. The precise nature of the reward should be appropriate to your past deeds, and the NPCs you have assisted during play or in your back story. The tokens of gratitude may vary from a single high-quality item, to a purse of silver (which will be available at the start of the next adventure). Of course, not all gifts are what they appear and the GM may attach an awkward complication to this sudden windfall.

#### **10–14: Somebody Else's Problem**

Whether through luck, good timing, or a fortunate misunderstanding, somebody else ends up dealing with a problem that would normally have landed on your doorstep. You find yourself with some unexpectedly free time. Gain 1 additional Endeavour to use this Downtime.

#### **15–19: A Quiet Week**

Nothing catches fire, the tax collector passes you over, and nobody accuses you of witchcraft. By the standards of the Empire, it has been an extraordinarily peaceful week. During the next adventure, you may ignore the first Fumble or Minor Miscast you would suffer.

#### **20–24: A Good Day's Work**

Fortune smiles upon your labours. Perhaps a satisfied customer recommends your services, your master is unusually generous with your wages, or your latest mark was particularly gullible. Should you take the *Income* Endeavour this Downtime, increase your Standing by 3 for the purposes of determining how much you earn.

#### **25–26: Is that an Original von Klapper?**

A bespectacled noble passing you on the street suddenly stops and exclaims with excitement. They are convinced that one of your Trappings (determined by the GM) is in fact a rare and important example of a particular artisan's work. You may immediately sell the Trapping to the hapless collector for twice its listed value.

#### **27–29: Morr's Embrace**

A relative, old friend, or mysterious benefactor dies and leaves you some money in their will. Gain some coin appropriate to your Status — Brass: 20 brass pennies; Silver: 10 silver shillings; Gold: 2 gold crowns. This windfall is received before completing Endeavours, so must be spent or banked before jealous family members, opportunistic cutpurses, or hidden burial fees whisk it away; see **Money to Burn** below.

#### **30–34: Forewarned is Forearmed**

You are given a cryptic omen by a dream, two-shilling mystic, or Celestial wizard you encounter on the street. During your next adventure, your maximum Fortune points are increased by 1.

#### **35–41: What a Bargain!**

While browsing the local markets, you come across an item tucked away in a stall that has clearly been overlooked by the merchant, who doesn't know the value of what they have. You may purchase a single Trapping for half its usual price.

#### **42–47: Pedlar**

A wizened, well-travelled pedlar who likes to gossip passes through. For the price of a flagon of ale (6 d) you may attempt the *Latest News* Endeavours without using one of your Endeavours.

#### **48–51: A Useful Connection**

Through your labours or a social encounter, you find yourself in the home of an accomplished artisan or scholar. Without requiring one of your Endeavours, you may choose one of the following options: undertake the Commission Endeavour, or undertake the Research Lore Endeavour, with Advantage on any associated Tests.

#### **52–56: Soothsayer**

You discover a small tent in a corner of the local market square, from which wafts the scent of incense and bitter herbs. A wizened crone beckons you inside and offers to read your fortune. For the price of 1 shilling, you may acquire the *Doomed* Talent, if you do not already have it, or otherwise roll on the table of *Doomings* (page 118) replacing your previous result with the new result. Should you try to locate the tent again so your companions can get a reading, you are mysteriously unable to find it.

#### <span id="page-194-0" data-folio="195"></span>**57–59: Arcane Auction**

The belongings of a recently deceased neighbour are auctioned, including a dusty, old book of unknown origin. If you are able to pay the 2 GC asking price, the book is yours! Beyond any other secrets the book may reveal, as determined by the GM, you (or any other Character of your choice who can read) may undertake the *Research Lore* Endeavour without using one of your Endeavours, making any required Test with Advantage.

#### **60–67: Oi! You spilled my pint!**

While carousing in a tavern, browsing the markets, attending a cult rite, or any appropriate activity, you run afoul of a hot-headed member of a local guild, criminal enterprise, or other organisation. The GM determines the faction in question and, during the next adventure, you suffer Disadvantage on social Tests with members of that group.

#### **68–74: Onerous Obligations**

A family wedding, guild meeting, temple festival, or some other unavoidable obligation demands your attention. By the time every duty has been discharged and every long-winded acquaintance has finished bending your ear, little time remains. You have 1 fewer Endeavour to use this Downtime.

#### **75–79: Impounded**

A zealous official decides one of your possessions requires inspection, a new licence, or proof of ownership. Recovering it means navigating the Empire's labyrinthine bureaucracy. Spend 1 Endeavour reclaiming one of your Trappings (chosen by the GM), or begin the next adventure without it.

#### **80–84: Ranald's Wrath**

Whether you cheated the wrong gambler or laughed at the wrong joke, the trickster god seems to have taken an interest in you… for the worse. Your clothes catch on each nail you pass, every ale you order is sour, and a pigeon relieves itself on your hat three times in one day. During the next adventure, your maximum Fortune points are decreased by 1.

#### **85–90: Scandalous Rumours**

Through no fault of your own — or perhaps entirely through your own doing — you become embroiled in a local scandal. Awkward conversations and sidelong glances follow wherever you go. During the next adventure, you cannot spend Fortune on social Tests.

#### **91–92: Betrayed!**

A friend, family member, or ally turns on you. Decide with the GM who betrayed you and why. During the next adventure, the betrayer or their agents may complicate your plans at least once. Whether or not you can make amends in some way is up to the GM.

#### **93–97: Accused Ally**

One of your allies (GM's choice) is implicated in a crime. A Character with a Law Guild Licence can use up one of their Endeavours to defend the ally on a successful **Average (+2 SL) Lore (Law)** Test. Otherwise, 3 Endeavours spent by the party will exonerate their ally (or, if guilty, concoct a convincing alibi). If the ally is freed, gain a *Major Favour* (see **Do Me a Favour** on page 200) for future use. If not, the ally faces punishment — fines, imprisonment, or even execution, as the GM sees fit.

#### **98–00: They're a Witch!**

A fearful local or an enemy with ulterior motives accuses you of witchcraft. You may spend an Endeavour and make a **Difficult (-1 SL) Charm** Test to attempt to clear your name. If you fail the Test or do not attempt to clear your name, a witch hunter takes interest in you. The GM may have them show up at inopportune moments during your adventures until you successfully clear your name. Killing the witch hunter will only confirm people's suspicions and set their allies upon you.

# **MONEY TO BURN**

After you've rolled on the **Character Events Table**, you can spend the money earned during your last adventures using the normal rules. After completing your Endeavours, all money held by your Character is considered spent. All of it. The silver lifted from a Burgher's purse? Gone. The booty liberated from pirates? Also gone. That Troll hoard you raided? Gone, too. All of it. What happened to it? It was spent, stolen, drunk, gambled, used for repairs, to pay off debts or taxes, given as a charitable donations or votive offerings, spent on bribes, or used in whatever other way you prefer. You should concoct the best story for what happens to those funds, as it explains a lot about your Character. If you want to keep some of your hard-won coin to use in the future, you should undertake the *Banking* Endeavour on page 197. If you want money at the beginning of the next adventure, honestly, or otherwise, depending on your Career, you should undertake the Income Endeavour on page 199.
