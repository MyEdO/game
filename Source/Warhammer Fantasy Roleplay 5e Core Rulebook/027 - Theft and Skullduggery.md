# <span id="page-133-0" data-folio="134"></span>**THEFT AND SKULLDUGGERY**

Crime is common in the Empire. Some say anything not nailed down is likely to be stolen, but that overlooks the thriving second-hand market for questionably acquired nails.

Characters are likely to break the law from time to time, often for good reason. A crime may even be the focus of an adventure, such as breaking into a wealthy merchant's home to uncover evidence of cult activity. In such cases, every detail matters, from breaking in and searching for hidden evidence to escaping unseen.

There is often no need to make a Test when time is not an issue or failure has few consequences. For example, if the players steal a locked chest, you can simply say they force it open once they have time, rather than requiring a Pick Lock Test.

#### **A TIME FOR CRIME**

If a Character earns their living through crime, such as a Thief or Charlatan, treat it like any other Career. Their routine work is resolved during Downtime using the *Income* Endeavour.

There is no need to roleplay picking the pocket of every NPC they encounter — that would quickly become frustrating for the other players!

# **Stealth and Surveillance**

Moving unseen and spotting danger are valuable skills throughout the Empire. Here are some common situations and how to resolve them.

#### **Keeping an Eye Out**

If a Character is watching for threats or suspects they are being followed, make an Opposed Perception Test against the target's appropriate Stealth Skill. The GM should allow the Test even if no one is there — a little paranoia is often healthy.

#### **Casing the Joint**

To observe a location, make a **Challenging (+0 SL) Perception**  Test. For each +SL, the GM reveals one useful fact, such as who comes and goes, how well the location is guarded, what lies inside, ways to enter or leave unseen, or whether anyone else is watching it.

#### **Sneaking Around**

To move unseen, make the appropriate Stealth Test (Rural, Underground, or Urban) opposed by the Perception of whoever you are trying to avoid. If there are multiple observers, use the highest Perception.

A suitable distraction, such as a modest fire, a street brawl, or a blathering guest (page 115), grants Advantage on Stealth Tests.

**COMMON FORMS OF SKULLDUGGERY**

| Action | Test |
|--------|------|
| Pick a pocket | A Sleight of Hand Test, opposed by your target's Perception. |
| Force open a door or window | A Strength Test to do it quickly, or a Stealth (Urban) Test with the right tools to do it quietly. |
| Pick a lock | A Pick Lock Test, with a difficulty set by the lock's quality. Especially challenging locks might require an Extended Test, with one roll every round, or every minute. |
| Connect with local criminals | A Difficult (-1 SL) Gossip Test, made with Advantage if you have the Criminal Talent yourself. |
| Argue your innocence | A Charm Test, opposed by your opponent's Cool, or an opposed Lore (Law) Test to do so in court. Either way, Test with Disadvantage if you were caught red-handed or if there is incriminating evidence. |
| Cheat at cards | A Sleight of Hand Test, opposed by your opponent's Perception. Success grants Advantage on your next Gamble Test during that game. |
| Bribe a guard | A Bribery Test to find someone open to being bribed, at a price set by their status. You might Haggle with them over the price. |
| Learn a thing's value | An Evaluate Test to learn something's value, or a Difficult (-1 SL) Evaluate Test to quickly spot the most valuable thing in a room. |

#### **EXAMPLE OUTCOMES**

**A Stealth (Rural) Test to infiltrate a guarded manor house.**

**Astounding Success (+5 SL):** You sneak inside and happen upon a servant's livery drying on the line — the perfect disguise should you wish to explore the living quarters.

**Impressive Success (+3 to +4 SL):** You sneak inside and notice a poorly guarded garden path you can later use to make a quick escape.

**Success (+1 to +2 SL):** You sneak inside the manor without being seen.

**Marginal Success (+0 SL):** You sneak inside, but leave signs of your entrance that will surely be noticed come morning.

**Marginal Failure (-0 SL):** You fail to find a safe way inside, but manage to avoid being spotted by the guards during your attempt.

**Failure (-1 to -2 SL):** You're discovered by a guard who spots you from across the courtyard. Time to run!

**Impressive Failure (-3 to -4 SL):** You stumble into the path of a patrol, who bellow a warning to the estate before drawing the blades and bearing down on you.

**Astounding Failure (-5 SL):** A misplaced step sees you tumbling from a watch house roof and right into the path of the captain of the guards, who recognises you on the spot.

#### **Silent Takedowns**

To silently subdue an unaware guard or sentry, make an Opposed Melee Test against the target's Endurance. If the attacker wins, they inflict damage equal to their Strength Bonus and the target gains the *Unconscious* Condition.

If the target wins, they instead gain the *Stunned* Condition and immediately raise the alarm.

Characters with the *Strike to Stun* Talent gain Advantage on this Test. Silent Takedowns can only be attempted outside combat against unaware targets.

# **Gambling and Cheating**

To resolve a game of chance, make an Opposed Gamble Test. The character with the highest SL wins.

To cheat, make a Sleight of Hand Test opposed by the highest Perception among your opponents. Success grants Advantage on your next Gamble Test in that game. Failure usually means you are caught.

Use these rules only when the outcome of the game is important to the adventure. Routine gambling for income is best handled with the *Income* Endeavour (page 199).

# <span id="page-135-0" data-folio="136"></span>**Traps**

From simple tripwires to pits filled with spikes, traps are laid across the Old World to hunt animals and deter unwanted interlopers. Encounters with traps may run from the mundane need to snare a brace of rabbits for dinner, to dealing with an ancient mechanism constructed to guard a Nehekharan tomb.

#### **Setting a Trap**

Setting and disarming simple snares while hunting for food is handled as part of an Outdoor Survival Test. Setting a trap to ensnare a specific target is done with a Set Trap Test, with a Difficulty determined by the complexity of the trap in question — see below. Failing a Set Trap Test is not usually dangerous, but if a Set Trap Test is Fumbled, there may be serious consequences as listed in the **Selection of Traps** table.

Unless specified otherwise, setting a trap takes about 20 minutes.

## **EXAMPLE OUTCOMES**

#### **A Sleight of Hand Test to cheat at cards.**

**Astounding Success (+5 SL):** You grant yourself enough cards to win any game you wish without making a Gamble Test at all. You could even frame another character for cheating, if you like!

**Impressive Success (+3 to +4 SL):** You pocket enough high cards to grant yourself Advantage on any Gamble Tests you make during the night's merriment.

**Success (+1 to +2 SL):** You pocket a pair of useful cards, granting you Advantage on your next Gamble Test.

**Marginal Success (+0 SL):** You gain Advantage on your next Gamble Test, but one of your opponents grows suspicious of you.

**Marginal Failure (-0 SL):** You pass off a failed palming of a card as mere clumsiness. Your opponents are all suspicious of you and watch you carefully from now on.

**Failure (-1 to -2 SL):** You are caught cheating by at least two of your opponents, who immediately call for your winnings to be confiscated, and you are kicked out into the frigid night.

**Impressive Failure (-3 to -4 SL):** Your cheating is obvious for all to see, and your opponents offer you two ways out — a night in the stocks, or a duel on the morrow.

**Astounding Failure (-5 SL):** A mistimed flourish sees pocketed cards spill from your coat for all to see. The bar erupts in an uproar, and a fight breaks out over who gets to gut you first.

#### **Spotting a Trap**

Spotting a trap requires a Perception Test. The Difficulty is determined by the result of the Set Trap Test made to deploy the mechanism.

If a Character specifically checks a small area, such as a door or hallway, for a trap, they gain Advantage on the associated Perception Test.

#### **Triggering a Trap**

If a Character fails to spot a trap and moves through the area it protects, the trap is triggered. Some traps may allow a Dodge Test to avoid any ill effects, but typically the unfortunate victim simply suffers consequences. In addition to any other effects, a Character affected by a trap also suffers the *Surprised* Condition.

#### **Disarming a Trap**

Once a Trap has been spotted, Disarming it requires a Set Trap Test with a Difficulty one step higher than that required to deploy it. You have Advantage on Tests to disarm a trap you set.

# **Breaking and Entering**

Characters will often want something others don't want them to have, or need to get somewhere they shouldn't. Locks, barred doors, and similar obstacles may deter casual opportunists, but rarely stop a determined intruder.

#### **Picking Locks**

Opening a lock without its key requires lockpicks (page 310). Improvised tools, such as a hairpin or nail, impose Disadvantage.

Picking a lock is an Extended Test (page 131), with one Test allowed each round. The lock determines the Difficulty and SL required.

If time is not an issue and discovery is unlikely, a character with the Pick Lock Skill and suitable tools can simply open the lock without making a Test.

#### <span id="page-137-0" data-folio="138"></span>**Forced Entry**

If Characters do not mind making noise, they can force open a locked door with a **Challenging (+0 SL) Melee** Test using a suitable implement (such as a mace, hammer, or any weapon with Encumbrance 2 or more) or a **Difficult (-1 SL) Strength** Test.

With appropriate tools, such as a crowbar (page 310), they can instead attempt a quiet entry with a **Challenging (+0 SL) Stealth** Test, opposed by an observer's Perception. Particularly sturdy barriers may increase the Difficulty.

# **Secret Signs**

Many organisations use coded signs to mark opportunities, warn of danger, or claim territory. Thieves, spies, and cultists often scratch subtle symbols onto doorways, signposts, or even coins to communicate with one another.

Make a **Challenging (+0 SL) Secret Signs (Thief )** Test to spot these signs, or to leave a message for others who understand the same signs.

# **Theft and Scams**

Relieving others of their belongings has always been a popular pursuit, and the Characters may find themselves taking part from time to time.

#### **Mugging**

Intimidating a victim into handing over their valuables requires an Intimidate Test opposed by the target's Cool. The Difficulty depends on the circumstances — a lone traveller on a forest road is far easier to intimidate than three stevedores on a busy Altdorf street. Escaping before the authorities arrive is usually a Stealth or Athletics Test.

#### **EXAMPLE OUTCOMES**

**A Secret Signs Test to gather information on local thieves**

**Astounding Success (+5 SL):** As below, and you find subtle signs designed to misdirect most, but which point to another thief 's cache. It contains a pouch of 1d10 Silver Shillings nestled between a crowbar and a length of rope.

**Impressive Success (+3 to +4 SL):** As below, and you also discover which streets are the most carefully patrolled by the town watch.

**Success (+1 to +2 SL):** You spot some signs pointing to a local bar frequented by the criminal underworld, where you can doubtless learn more.

**Marginal Success (+0 SL):** You spot signs indicating the way to a local bar, but draw undue attention from someone else — a wary burgher, or perhaps a fellow thief.

**Marginal Failure (-0 SL):** You fail to spot any hidden signs, but a local vagabond notices what you're doing. Laughing, he points you in the right direction, but spreads word that you're somewhat of a fool.

**Failure (-1 to -2 SL):** You spot nothing, but avoid drawing undue attention to yourself.

**Impressive Failure (-3 to -4 SL):** You misread a sign and end up in a bar frequented by the town watch.

**Astounding Failure (-5 SL):** You completely misread some signs and find yourself stumbling into the den of the most notorious and least welcoming gang boss in town.

#### **Picking Pockets**

Picking a pocket requires a Sleight of Hand Test opposed by the victim's Perception. A Test is only required when the theft is important to the adventure, such as stealing a jailer's keys. Routine theft is best handled with the *Income* Endeavour during Downtime.

#### <span id="page-138-0" data-folio="139"></span>**EXAMPLE OUTCOMES**

**A Charm Test to convince a merchant to invest their coin with you**

**Astounding Success (+5 SL):** The merchant invests ten gold crowns in your scheme and convinces an even more credulous business partner to invest ten more.

**Impressive Success (+3 to +4 SL):** The merchant is so impressed by your scheme that he immediately places ten gold crowns with you.

**Success (+1 to +2 SL):** The merchant happily parts with the five gold crowns you sought.

**Marginal Success (+0 SL):** You convince your mark to invest a gold crown, but he'd like to invite his brother into the opportunity as well, who just so happens to be an officer in the town watch.

**Marginal Failure (-0 SL):** You fail to secure the coin, but the merchant isn't aware you were trying to con him.

**Failure (-1 to -2 SL):** The merchant passes on your investment and advises colleagues to avoid you.

**Impressive Failure (-3 to -4 SL):** The merchant sees right through your scam and reports you to the town watch.

**Astounding Failure (-5 SL):** The merchant avoids your obvious scam, but recognises your enthusiasm, if not your talent. He offers you a deal — help him con his partner, or he will report you to the city watch himself.

#### **Criminal Coinage**

Counterfeit currency is common, often involving coating a base metal, such as lead, in a thin layer of gold or silver. Real currency is sometimes debased by clipping, removing a small amount of precious metal from the edges. Merchants are on the lookout for such things, and a successful Evaluate Test reveals if a payment contains any obviously dodgy currency.

> When forging coinage, a **Hard (-2 SL) Art (Engraving) Test** is made to create a convincing die, followed by a Trade (Smith) Test to strike the coins. Unless relevant to the events of an adventure, this is best handled via the *Income* Endeavour (page 199).

#### **Fraud and Charlatanry**

Routine scams, such as selling dubious cures, are almost indistinguishable from more honest forms of commerce and usually resolved during Downtime, unless it is relevant to the adventure.

More ambitious cons require a Charm Test opposed by the victim's Cool, with the Difficulty determined by how believable the deception is. Selling a fake cure to a desperate victim might be Average (+2 SL), while persuading a canny merchant to invest 100 Gold Crowns in a dubious venture in distant Lustria could be Very Hard (-3 SL).
