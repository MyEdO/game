# <span id="page-38-0" data-folio="39"></span>**5. TALENTS, TRAPPINGS, AND FINAL GAME DETAILS**

Talents are your special abilities, tricks, and techniques with individual game impacts (see page 114). **Choose a Talent from the first level of your Career and add it to your Character Sheet.**

Trappings are your equipment, clothes, weapons, and other carried items.

- All Characters begin with Clothing, a Dagger, and a Pouch.
- You also begin play with all of the Trappings from your Career's first level.
- Finally, you start with some Trappings common to your Class, as shown in the Class Trappings table.

You begin the game with an amount of coins decided by your Status:

**STARTING WEALTH**

| Status Tier | Wealth                                           |
|----------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Brass          | 20 brass pennies plus 2d10 per Standing Level    |
| Silver         | 10 silver shillings plus 1d10 per Standing Level |
| Gold           | 2 gold crowns plus 1 per Standing Level          |

Record each of these items on your Character Sheet. If you end up with two of something, keep the spare! You can always sell it if you wish, and a spare weapon can often come in handy.

So, if you were Brass 3, you would start with 6d10+20 Brass pennies; if Silver 3, you would begin with 3d10+10 silver shillings, and if you were Gold 3, you would have 5 gold crowns.

Money can be used to purchase more Trappings or saved for spending during play. Commonly available items and the relative worth of different coins are covered in **Chapter 11: Consumer Guide**.

# <span id="page-39-0" data-folio="40"></span>**Wounds**

Wounds show how much Damage you can take before you suffer serious injury.

**Wounds = Strength Bonus (SB) + Willpower Bonus (WPB) + 2 × Toughness Bonus (TB)**

So, if you have **3**4 Strength, **2**7 Willpower and **3**8 Toughness, your Wounds would be 3+2+3+3=11. If you have the Hardy Talent, add your Toughness Bonus again.

Write your Wounds total on your Character Sheet.

# **Movement (M)**

Movement is used to determine your Walk and Run speed. If using a grid for movement, this indicates how many squares you can move in a Round. If not using a grid, it indicates how many inches across the tabletop. When you Run, you double these distances.

**Example:** *A human with a Movement of 4 can walk 4 squares or run 8 squares per Round on a grid. If not using a grid, they can walk 4 inches or run 8 inches per Round.*

Over the longer term it indicates how many miles per hour you can comfortably travel on foot. For more on Movement, see page 156, and 162 for moving in comabt.

**MOVEMENT DISTANCES**

| Movement | Walk (Yards) | Run (Yards) |
|----------|--------------|-------------|
| 3        | 6            | 12          |
| 4        | 8            | 16          |
| 5        | 10           | 20          |

Write your Movement total on your Character Sheet.

# **Maximum Encumbrance**

This measures how much you can comfortably carry without being slowed down and tired out.

**Maximum Encumbrance = Strength Bonus (SB) + Toughness Bonus (TB)**

Dwarfs, and Characters with the Sturdy Talent, double this figure.

You must account for the Encumbrance of any items you routinely carry around with you, reducing the Encumbrance value of any worn items by 1. The rules for Encumbrance, and the effects of being over-encumbered, are listed on page 299.

# **Oh Fickle Fate**

Some say that gods reward those who allow their hand to shape lives and guide actions. If you chose to accept the first random result for each of your Species, Career, and Characteristics, increase your Fate by 1.

# **CREATING A MAGICIAN OR PRIEST**

If you are creating a Character who is a spellcaster or miracle worker of some sort, bear in mind that you will need to meet a short list of minimum requirements in order to work your magic from the start of your adventuring career.

A spellcaster has various options in order to cast a Spell, such as reading from grimoires, but a sensible starting suite of abilities would include:

- The *Second Sight* Talent, without which you may not perceive the Winds of Magic.
- At least one advance in the Language (Magick) Skill.
- At least one advance in the Channelling Skill, with a specialisation appropriate to the type of magic you wish to work with. For example, a Wizard using the Lore of Shadows would need Channelling (*Ulgu*).
- The *Petty Magic* Talent, in order to begin learning spells to cast.

This may be quite a lot to acquire as a starting character, but once you have the necessary Skills and Talents you will be able to increase your repertoire of spells when you gain experience. See **Chapter 8: Magic** for details of the effects of Spells and how to cast them.

Priests have a slightly easier time, reflecting the fact that their Blessings and Miracles are not as varied and powerful as Spells. In order to begin play as a Priest capable of working Blessings a Character will need:

- The *Bless (Deity)* Talent, appropriate to the god you have chosen to worship.
- At least one advance in the Pray Skill.

See **Chapter 7: Religion and Belief** for details of Blessings, and how to work Miracles.
