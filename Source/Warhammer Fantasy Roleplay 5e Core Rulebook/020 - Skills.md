### <span id="page-108-0" data-folio="109"></span>**SKILLS**

There are three types of Skill:

- **Basic Skills** can be used by anyone, even without any Advances.
- **Advanced Skills** can only be used if you have at least one Advance in the Skill.
- **Grouped Skills** are groups of related skills, each one covering a particular area of specialisation, eg. Lore (Empire) and Lore (Tilea).

### **Basic and Advanced Skills**

Basic Skills are those that anyone might practice in day-to-day life, without specialised training. Basic Skills can be Tested even if you have taken no Advances in them, using the Characteristic associated with the Skill.

**Example:** *Sigrid has no Advances in* Athletics*. However, as*  Athletics *is a Basic Skill, she can still Test* Athletics *using the Characteristic associated with it:* Agility*.*

<span id="page-109-0" data-folio="110"></span>Advanced Skills require specialist knowledge, training, or firsthand experience to even attempt to use. You may only Test an Advanced Skill if you have taken at least one Advance in it.

**Example:** *Adhemar has an* Intelligence *of 36 and no Advances in either the Basic* Gamble *Skill or the Advanced* Heal *Skill, both based on* Intelligence*. He can Test the* Gamble *Skill at 36 as it is Basic, and anyone can Test Basic Skills. The* Heal *Skill is an Advanced Skill, so Adhemar has no idea how to patch up wounds and can do nothing more useful to help an injured comrade than screaming loudly for a doctor!*

### **Grouped Skills and Specialisation**

Grouped Skills are an array of related Skills gathered together under one heading. Each related Skill is called a Specialisation. When a Specialisation is mentioned in the rules, it is marked in brackets.

***Example:*** Language *is a Grouped Skill that represents many related Language Skills under a single Skill heading. Its Specialisations include* Language (Bretonnian)*,* Language (Magick)*, and*  Language (Mootish)*.* 

When you gain an Advance in a Grouped Skill, you must allocate the Advance to an appropriate Specialisation. Sometimes the Specialisation options will be marked clearly in your Career, so you simply select one of the options on offer. In other cases — such as when a Specialisation is marked as 'Any One', meaning you can choose one Specialisation — you will need to select a Specialisation yourself.

**Example:** *Theodora has a choice of* Lore (Any One) *in her Career. When she allocates an Advance to that Skill, she has to decide which*  Lore *Specialisation to take. After some deliberation, she chooses to Advance her* Lore (Theology)*, showing she has improved her understanding of religious matters.*

Other than that, Grouped Skills are handled in exactly the same way as normal Skills, with each Specialisation handled like a unique Skill.

**Example:** *Sigrid has 1 Advance in the Basic Skill* Entertain (Singing)*, but nothing in* Entertain (Acting)*. This means she Tests her* Fellowship *of 41 when attempting to act, and Tests 46 when singing (41 + 5 = 46). By comparison, Theodora has a single Advance in the Advanced Skill* Animal Training (Pigeon)*, but no Advances in* Animal Training (Horse)*. This means she can use the Skill to train her pigeons to drop bombs on unsuspecting enemies, but she cannot train her horse to charge into a formation of enemy troops, as she has no idea how to train horses.*

### <span id="page-110-0" data-folio="111"></span>**Animal Care (Int)** *advanced*

Care for animals and heal them from disease or injury. A Character with an Advance in this Skill can keep animals healthy under normal conditions without needing to Test. A Test is required to diagnose or resolve problems with animals, such as spotting signs of illness or healing an injury (page 171).

### **Animal Training (Int)** *advanced, grouped*

**Specialisations:** Demigryph, Dog, Hawk, Horse, Pigeon

Your understanding of a particular type of animal, and ability to train them. To train a beast, undertake the *Animal Training*  Endeavour between adventures (see page 196).

### **Art (Dex)** *basic, grouped*

**Specialisations:** Drawing, Engraving, Mosaics, Painting, Sculpture, Tattooing, Weaving, Writing.

Create works of art in your chosen medium. The more SL achieved, the better the quality of the final piece. For complicated or large works of art, an Extended Test may be required.

### **Athletics (Ag)** *basic*

Your ability to run, jump, move or perform any other general physical activity.

### **Bribery (Fel)** *basic*

Find someone who is willing to accept a bribe, such as a guild notary to accept forged paperwork or a gate guard to look the other way while you slip inside after nightfall. You can also make a Bribery Test to judge if a particular person could be bribed, and what their price is likely to be.

### **Channelling (WP)** *advanced, grouped*

**Specialisations Include:** *Aqshy*, *Azyr*, *Chamon*, *Dhar*, *Ghur*, *Ghyran*, *Hysh*, *Magick*, *Shyish*, *Ulgu*.

Ability to call upon and control the various Winds of Magic, solely used with the magic rules. See **Chapter 8: Magic** for details.

### **Charm (Fel)** *basic*

Makes people think favourably of you, your opinions, and actions you propose. People won't necessarily do what you say simply because you've passed a Charm Test, but they will at least give you due consideration.

### **Charm Animal (WP)** *basic*

Your aptitude for befriending, quickly calming, or subjugating animals. A Charm Animal Test can convince a wild animal to back off, or a guard dog to devour a proffered scrap of meat rather than your arm.

### **Climb (S)** *basic*

The ability to ascend steep or vertical surfaces, like a perilous mountainside or the rain-slick wall of a tumbledown townhouse. Climbing can sometimes be used in combat when facing especially monstrous foes — see page 162 for combat movement.

### **Consume Alcohol (T)** *basic*

How well you can handle alcohol without letting it cloud your judgment or render you senseless. Often used to impress those who find the ability to down an unreasonable number of beverages to be praiseworthy.

### **Cool (WP)** *basic*

Allows you to remain calm under stress, resist fear in the face of horror, and stick to your convictions. It is generally used to resist the attempts of others to Charm or Intimidate you, or to curtail other Psychological effects (see page 183).

### **Dodge (Ag)** *basic*

Avoid things through ducking, diving, and moving quickly, and is used extensively to sidestep falling rocks, incoming weapons, and the like. In combat, Dodge is generally used to resist attacks or avoid damage.

### **Drive (Ag)** *basic*

Maneuvering vehicles — most commonly simple carts and lumbering coaches, but also the more experimental creations of Imperial Engineers — along the roads of the Empire with as little incident as possible.

### **Endurance (T)** *basic*

Called upon when you must endure hardship, withstand deprivation, stand without moving for long periods of time, or survive harsh environments. In particular, Endurance is Tested to resist or recover from various Conditions (see page 184) and helps you recover lost Wounds (see page 170).

### **Entertain (Fel)** *basic, grouped*

**Specialisations:** Acting, Comedy, Singing, Storytelling. Delight crowds with the spoken word, perhaps by singing, acting, or attempting a few jokes — hopefully entertaining all those within earshot.

### **Evaluate (Int)** *advanced*

Lets you determine the value of goods. Everybody is assumed to know the relative worth of general items, but Evaluate allows you to identify the value of curious and unique items, recognise forgeries, or spot flaws.

### <span id="page-111-0" data-folio="112"></span>**Gamble (Int)** *basic*

Measure the likelihood that a bet will pay off, as well as successfully engage in various games of chance. If you wish to improve your chances through cheating, see page 135.

### **Gossip (Fel)** *basic*

Ferret out interesting and useful news, and spread rumours of your own. Best practised at a town square, inn, well, or other gathering place.

### **Haggle (Fel) basic**

Secure better deals when negotiating with others, when purchasing goods for example. Typically involves an Opposed Haggle Test (page 131).

### **Heal (Int)** *advanced*

You have been trained to deal with injuries and diseases. A successful Heal Test allows you to diagnose illness, treat a disease, heal wounds or staunch a *Bleeding* Condition. Full rules for illness, injury, and treatments can be found on page 172.

### **Intimidate (S)** *basic*

Coerce or frighten sentient creatures — generally with an overt threat, though sometimes a subtle implication or even a look is enough.

### **Intuition (I)** *basic*

Gives you a feel for your surroundings, to notice when something is wrong, and read people to sense when they are lying or hiding something.

### **Language (Int)** *advanced, grouped*

**Specialisations:** Bretonnian, Reikspiel, Wastelander, and many more. See page 149.

Access to extra languages beyond your native tongue. A single Advance is enough to make yourself understood in that language, but a Test may be required to comprehend complicated discussions. Once you have 4 Advances, you count as fluent and no longer need to make Tests.

### **Leadership (Fel)** *basic*

Your ability to lead others and command respect. While most often associated with martial situations, a resolute leader can quickly direct a fast response to a fire or other such calamity, and nobles use the Skill frequently to command their lessers.

### **Lore (Int)** *advanced, grouped*

**Specialisations:** Engineering, Geology, Heraldry, History, Law, Local\*, Magic, Metallurgy, Science, Theology.

Knowledge of a subject you have been taught or have first-hand knowledge of. Basic facts related to a specific Lore Skill can be recalled easily, with a Test required only to recall obscure or unusual information.

### **Melee (WS)** *basic, grouped*

**Specialisations:** Basic, Brawling, Cavalry, Fencing, Flail, Parry, Polearm, Two-handed.

Training and experience with a single type of close combat weaponry. Each Melee Specialisation indicates expertise in using a specific class of weapon. If you do not have the correct Specialisation for a weapon, you will usually suffer a penalty when using it — see **Chapter 11: Consumer Guide** for more.

### **Navigation (I)** *basic*

Allows you to find your way in the wilderness, or across the ocean, using landmarks, stellar bodies, charts, or maps.

### **Outdoor Survival (Int)** *basic*

Used to survive in the wild, including the ability to fish, hunt, forage, and build fires and shelters. Experienced survivors are practiced at reading the signs of incoming inclement weather, making do with minimal tools, and noticing the spoor of local beasts.

### <span id="page-112-0" data-folio="113"></span>**Perception (I)** *basic*

Your ability to notice things with your senses — sight, smell, hearing, touch, taste, and any other senses you may possess, such as magical or inhuman senses.

### **Perform (Ag)** *advanced, grouped*

**Specialisations:** Acrobatics, Clowning, Dancing, Firebreathing, Juggling, Miming, Rope Walking.

You have learned a physically demanding art, perhaps as a way to make a living. A successful Perform Test lets you entertain those near enough to see you.

### **Pick Lock (Dex)** *advanced*

You know the mechanisms of locks and how to open them without their proper keys. Many locks are simple affairs made to vex only untutored thieves, while others can be complex dwarf-made mechanisms capable of frustrating all but the most determined plunderer.

### **Play (Dex)** *advanced, grouped*

**Specialisations:** Bagpipe, Lute, Harpsichord, Horn, Violin. Make music with an instrument, hopefully well enough to impress others. A successful Play Test lets you entertain those near enough to hear you.

### **THE OLD WORLD'S A STAGE…**

While there is some overlap between the Skills of Entertain, Perform, and Play, each reflects a distinct group of Skills.

**Entertain** is a Basic Skill using Fellowship. It represents those Skills of entertainment that anyone can attempt, even without training, such as singing or telling stories.

**Perform** is an Advanced Skill using Agility. Perform Specialisations require training to attempt, and rely on physical speed and coordination.

**Play** is also an Advanced Skill, this time using Dexterity. It represents training in specific musical instruments, all of which need teaching and practice to perform well.

You should choose carefully when determining whether your chosen Specialisation belongs to Entertain, Perform, or Play; consider how the Character will physically enact the Skill, and whether an untrained beginner could do so with any success. After all, someone without any musical training could feasibly sing beautifully, but hand them a set of bagpipes…

### **Pray (Fel)** *advanced*

Invoke, appeal to, or otherwise commune with a deity. For more information on using the Pray Skill to seek divine intervention, see **Chapter 7: Religion and Belief**.

### **Ranged (BS)** *advanced, grouped*

**Specialisations:** Blackpowder, Bow, Crossbow, Engineering, Entangling, Explosives, Sling, Throwing.

Anyone can throw a rock, but it takes training and practice to use weapons like bows, pistols, and throwing knives. Each Ranged Specialisation is specific to a group of ranged weapons. If you do not have the Ranged Specialisation for a weapon you wish to use, refer to **Chapter 11: Consumer Guide** for the penalties you will suffer when using the weapon.

### **Research (Int)** *advanced*

Finding useful and frequently obscure knowledge in libraries and other such storehouses of information. Research requires you to have the *Read/Write* Talent (see page 124). More on what kinds of information you can find and where to start looking can be found on page 150.

### **Ride (Ag)** *basic, grouped*

**Specialisations:** Demigryph, Griffon, Horse, Pegasus. Proficiency at riding a particular group of animals; Ride (Horse), for example, will let you ride horses, mules, donkeys, and similar. See page 168 for rules on fighting from the back of a mount.

### **Row (S)** *basic*

Your prowess at pulling an oar to move a boat through the water. A Row Test is typically only needed when racing, navigating rapids, desperately avoiding bog octopuses, or similar challenging feats.

### **Sail (Ag)** *advanced*

Operate and manoeuvre a sailing vessel — including knotwork, steering, gauging the wind, and more. Sail is required to operate your own vessel, either alone or as part of a crew. If you are commanding a well trained crew, use Leadership instead.

### **LOCAL LORES**

When you take the Lore (Local) skill, choose a locality that your Character is familiar with, eg. The Reikland, Tilea, Ubersreik, or The Street of a Thousand Taverns. If you take this Skill at character creation, decide with the GM what this should be — it could be your home village, or another place you are familiar with.

### <span id="page-113-0" data-folio="114"></span>**Secret Signs (Int)** *advanced, grouped*

**Specialisations:** Grey Order, Guild (Any One), Ranger, Scout, Thief, Vagabond.

You have been taught how to use clandestine markings only intelligible to members of a select group. Vagabonds might indicate which homeowners are likely to offer alms, thieves may want to indicate territorial markings or warnings, while scouts may want to warn one another about a dangerous monster's lair nearby (see page 138).

### **Set Trap (Dex)** *advanced*

From a simple snare or bear trap, to more spectacular devices like Von Grizzel's Thief-render, players are unlikely to venture far in the Old World without encountering traps. The Set Trap Skill measures your ability to set and disarm traps of all varieties.

### **Sleight of Hand (Dex)** *advanced*

Your ability to pick pockets, palm objects, and perform minor tricks of prestidigitation, as well as cheat at games of chance. This Skill is typically Opposed by the Perception Skill of your target.

### **Stealth (Ag)** *basic, grouped*

**Specialisations:** Rural, Underground, Urban.

Move and hide without being detected, blend into a crowd, or otherwise avoid drawing attention to yourself.

### **Swim (S)** *advanced*

Moving and staying afloat in water. If you have the Swim Skill, you are automatically presumed to be able to swim freely without a Test. But if you find yourself in difficult currents, racing, or fleeing from a ravenous stirpike, a Test will be required.

### **Track (I)** *advanced*

Your ability to follow subtle trails left by others. Track is used to follow and find your target, as well as to hide your trail.

### **Trade (Dex)** *advanced, grouped*

**Specialisations:** Apothecary, Calligrapher, Chandler, Carpenter, Cook, Embalmer, Engineer, Herbalist, Shipwright, Smith, Tanner.

Your ability to practice a trade, to create something or provide a service, as well as your knowledge of the relevant lore surrounding your trade.
