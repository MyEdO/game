# <span id="page-201-0" data-folio="202"></span>**GODS OF THE EMPIRE**

The people of the Old World recognise many deities. Some are worshipped across the whole of the Old World, some are restricted to one nation or region, and some are patrons of just a single town or occupation. In the Empire, the pantheon of gods is split into three broad categories: the Old Gods, the Classical Gods, and the Provincial Gods. Standing apart from these is Sigmar, the first emperor, and patron deity of the Empire as a whole.

#### **The Old Gods**

The Old Gods, occasionally called the Country Gods or Northern Gods, refer to a family of deities who have been worshipped in the Empire since it was blanketed by unbroken forest and populated by wandering barbarian tribes. Many Old Gods stood as patrons to one or more of the tribes, and to this day some are still associated with the old territories of those ancient peoples. Although few say so out loud, many citizens of the Empire regard the Old Gods as the true deities of the Empire, and other gods as relative newcomers.

As time passed, four gods rose to prominence amongst the Old Gods, worshipped by dominant cults spread from one end of the Empire to the other: Ulric, Taal, Rhya, and Manann, representing the primal spheres of war, nature, fertility, and the seas.

#### **The Classical Gods**

The Classical Gods, occasionally called the Town Gods or Southern Gods, spread from the southern lands of Tilea and Estalia through trade, diplomatic contact, and war. Today, their worship is popular in the cosmopolitan towns and cities, and some nobles and townsfolk privately regard them as more sophisticated than the Old Gods.

The most widespread cults of Classical Gods in the Empire are dedicated to Verena, Myrmidia, Shallya, and Morr, patrons of wisdom, strategy, mercy, and death.

#### **The Provincial Gods**

The Empire is home to a vast array of deities — patrons of provinces, towns, forests, lakes, rivers, crafts, and much more besides. Local legends and myths weave these Provincial Gods into complex pantheons. Most are served by only small, local cults and wield little influence. There are exceptions, however. Towering above the others, Handrich, the god of trade, has grown alongside commerce and now boasts a significant cult presence among the Empire's rising merchant class.

#### **Ranald**

The god of rogues and trickery has an uncomfortable position in the Old World pantheon. His worship is frowned upon, if not outright proscribed, by many in positions of power, who see his adherents as little more than thieves and revolutionaries. For all that people may publicly decry the worship of Ranald, they mutter a quiet prayer to him when they need a little luck on their side.

#### **Sigmar**

Sigmar founded the Empire over two thousand years ago, and his legend recounts how he conquered unthinkable foes and overcame impossible odds. Reigning for fifty years, he eventually abdicated and turned east to return his magical warhammer, Ghal Maraz, to its forgers: his old allies, the dwarfs. He was never seen again. Not long after, oracles and prophets claimed Sigmar had ascended to godhood, invested by Ulric before the entire pantheon of gods old and new.

Today, many centuries later, the Cult of Sigmar, patron of the Empire, has spread to such an extent that its leader, the grand theogonist, is arguably more powerful than the emperor himself.

# <span id="page-202-0" data-folio="203"></span>**Other Pantheons**

The different countries and species of the Old World all have deities of their own. Some, according to theologians, are aspects of other deities worshipped under different names. Others are particularly revered by a particular species — examples include Grungni, the dwarf ancestor god of mining and craftsmanship; Isha, a goddess of fertility and nature who is seen as the mother of all elves; and Esmerelda, the halfling goddess of hearth, home, and family.

### **The Chaos Gods**

The daemonic gods of the Realms of Chaos are the greatest threat to the Old World, each determined to bring absolute ruin to the mortal realm. Their worship by lost and damned souls is pervasive and clandestine, with uncounted dark cultists infiltrating all levels of society. Khorne, Nurgle, Tzeentch, and Slaanesh: Chaos Gods of slaughter, decrepitude, ambition, and indulgence. Few dare whisper their twisting names, for they harbour malevolent power, and leave mutation and horror in their wake.
