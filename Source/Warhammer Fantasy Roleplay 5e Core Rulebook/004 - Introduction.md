# *'So, what's drawn you to my door, wastrel?*

*You had a quiet life before, I'd wager, mucking out a pigsty or kneeling at the feet of nobles for scraps. What fancy carried you thence? Adventure? Gold? Justice? Hah! You might as well confess to madness and be done.* 

*Adventure is what fools call the miseries they've endured. Justice is a lie, though blood can be had in its place, if you've the stomach for it. Gold, on the other hand… that I can promise, provided you've the grit to earn it, and the wit not to ask too many questions…'*

These are the darkest of years, an era that prophets name the End Times. Karaz Ankor, the ancient empire of the dwarfs, is all but overrun. The elves have withdrawn to distant lands or cavernous forests. The Empire stands, but its foes muster in ever greater numbers, and nefarious cults plot and scheme within its borders. The Emperor Karl Franz, anointed to his position in 2502 IC by the will of the elector counts and the blessings of mighty Sigmar, rules with strength and wisdom that some nevertheless deem insufficient to the task.

Welcome to **Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay**, a game like no other. Here you will explore a world of glittering wonders, vile darkness, and everything in between. You will encounter feared and respected wizards who master their arts in the high towers of the Colleges of Magic, as well as witches who dabble in magics they barely understand. Priests, virtuous and fanatical in uneven measure, preach the word of the gods and caution against dark powers they fear to name. Proud ranks of soldiers march out to cheers and wage war on orcs, beastmen, and other invaders. They return scarred and diminished, or do not return at all.

Our protagonists are no heroes, assured of victory, gold, and glory. What they would gain, they must win, often against the odds, by strength, wit, and fortune. Coin is hard-earned and quickly spent, and the most lucrative opportunities pit would-be adventurers against hidden cults, bloodthirsty mercenaries, or inhuman beasts that dwell in dark forests or deep beneath the earth. Magic provides a powerful advantage, but it bears risks of its own, and there is no pyre so well fed that a witch hunter could not fit one more cur atop it. The gods heed the prayers of their most faithful, but they are jealous and fickle, quick to withdraw their blessing from those they no longer consider worthy.

This book contains everything you need, aside from a few dice and some paper and pencils. So gather your friends, appoint a gamemaster, and prepare to explore the grim and perilous world of **Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay**.

### **NEW TO ROLEPLAYING GAMES**

**Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay** is a tabletop roleplaying game, sometimes shortened to RPG or TTRPG, that combines elements of storytelling with board game mechanics. Most players will create a Character, one of the protagonists of the story. One player in your group will take on the role of Gamemaster (GM), who narrates the events and plays all of the other characters the players might meet.

If you are new to RPGs, you can find more information, free adventures, and other releases on our website, www.cubicle7games.com.

## **THROWING BONES**

Games of **Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay** use ten-sided dice marked from 0–9, where a roll of 0 counts as a result of 10. These are called d10s, and the number of dice to roll is shown as 1d10, 2d10, 3d10, and so on.

When rolling multiple dice, add the results together. For example, a roll of 0 and 3 on 2d10 gives a result of 13 (10 + 3).

Sometimes, a die roll will be modified by adding or subtracting a number. For example, 1d10+4 means roll one d10 and add 4, while 2d10-3 means roll two d10s, total the results, then subtract 3.

The rules also use 1d100, a roll from 1–100 made with two d10s. Designate one die as the tens die and the other as the units die, then read the results as a two-digit number. A 1 on the tens die and 4 on the units die gives 14; a 4 and 2 gives 42. If both dice roll 0, the result is 100.

In general, low rolls give successful results. So, whether you're rolling a d10 or a d100, you want to get as close to 01 as possible.

<span id="page-7-0" data-folio="8"></span><span id="page-8-0" data-folio="9"></span><span id="page-9-0" data-folio="10"></span>Being the thoughts of Frederich 'Olde' Weirde of Altdorf, Noted Scholar, Professor of Esoteric Studies, Historian, Antiquarian, Fellow of the Altdorf Men's Historical Society and Connoisseur of Finest Estalian Port, upon the current state of affairs of our great nation.

The return of young Stefan from his trip to Sylvania ought to have proved cause for significant celebration in the Weirde household but, unfortunately, it coincided with a fresh outbreak of Frau Weirde's chronic weevil cough. Given the great sum I have wasted on visits to the physician and donations to the temple-hospice of merciful Shallya, I am at the end of my patience with it, and so, rather than hark at her hacking splutter through the walls of our home, I decided to repair to the Hanging Crow to impart upon the boy a few of the habits proper to a learned scholar. Specifically, how a draught of Owd Badger's assists academic lore to trickle through the brain and better form new insights.

Alas, I neglected to consider that the quality of such insights depends on the contents of the trickled-through brain. The boy, who is bright but short of experience, began to expound on the sort of heedless prospectus outlined by radical thinkers and the sort of firebrand agitators one finds all too common at university salons.

I am no stranger to radical thought, I have disabused many a stuffy academic of their preconceptions, but arguments for tearing down the institutions of the Empire and starting again are invariably simplistic, cynical, self-serving attempts to further the opiners' own notoriety and coffers.

Need we even articulate that being able to idle away time in drinking dens rehearsing such nonsense is possible only because of the protection provided by the very same institutions they would abolish. Calling such deluded ranting academic study is enough to make Verena herself weep.

Owd Badger's growing augmentation of my intellect only confirmed the necessity of disabusing young Stefan of such naive and immature notions, lest he become some kind of cliched student agitator. I present here a verbatim written account of my Definitive Treatise on Our Great Nation, and urge all to use it in the education of their own feckless young.

As ever, your servant in pursuit of knowledge and wisdom, Olde Weirde

# <span id="page-10-0" data-folio="11"></span>Our Great Nation

Despite the bleatings of scholarship's lightweight parasites, it is plainly ludicrous to suggest that the Empire is anything but the pinnacle of civilisation. We are safe in our homes, protected not just by mighty armies but by Sigmar himself. There is usually plenty of food, and in times of scarcity our rulers intervene to alleviate hardship. We are free to pursue our hearts' desires, moderated only by our abilities, funds, and the laws of man and gods.

Those self-same laws protect the common man from criminals, malcontents and the depredations of any who would wield power improperly. To take all this for granted and demand we throw it away over a few details that aren't to your liking is tantamount to allowing goblin raiders into the fortress because the gate hinges need oiling. Sigmar himself would expel you from his realm for such poor judgement. Even for the poorest citizen, a strong Empire provides a life immeasurably better than if it was not there to protect and nurture them.

Look only to the Border Princes for confirmation. They are free of our 'oh-so-oppressive' laws! Free to starve! Free to die at the hands of the first orc warband to wander past! Free to toil unto death at the whim of the bully with the band of thugs declaring himself to be the High Prince of This Rock Upon Which We Stand!

And if you think the Kingdom of Bretonnia has anything to teach us on the rights of mankind, you have read too many of those bilgewater romance pamphlets.

The Emperor looks over his people like a shepherd in the Habercrybs looks over their flock. He builds a prosperous state like a master joiner builds an Altdorf townhouse, and jousts verbally with foreign ambassadors like a Knight Panther jousts with a goblin raider. His strength is mighty and inexhaustible, supported by the steadfast elector counts ruling the Empire's grand provinces, each in turn backed by the barons, counts, and lesser nobility. Our laws and glorious institutions such as the Council of State and Prime Estates ensure that no petty tyrant can subvert the machinery of government to their own ends. At least not for long, and not without fearing the hammer of Imperial justice.

There is, too, something to be said for the clarity of purpose to be found in the Empire. Every man and woman has their place, well laid out and easily understood, and therefore knows precisely where they may aspire to rise. A peasant works his land, a craftsman learns his trade, and a soldier serves his regiment. There is considerable comfort in knowing what is expected of you and what you may expect from others. Ours is no land of fancy, where the commoner imagines themself a worthy, nor where the nobly born must shovel their own turnips.

This does not mean that advancement is impossible, as the more excitable critics of our social order so often claim. Quite the contrary! A person may rise through diligence, good fortune, and the appropriate number of influential acquaintances to become a figure of genuine importance. The dutiful apprentice may become a master craftsman, the loyal soldier an officer, and the canny pedlar a wealthy merchant. Such advancement is all the more satisfying because it is truly earned.

Even those who have little may take pride in the greatness of the society that surrounds them. Why, even the beggars haunting the streets of Altdorf do so in gratitude that they have such wonderful, cobbled edifices on which to ply their trade.

And then there are the inns. Glorious institutions! They represent society and its best and can be found in every city, town, fly-blown village, and even along the roads of the Empire. Here the day's work may be put aside in favour of a warm hearth, a good drink, and agreeable company. Where else might a rat catcher share a bench with a roadwarden, a Tilean merchant debate the works of Hieronymous of Nuln with an Imperial student, or a fisherwoman best an engineer at a game of Whist? For a few hours, at least, we are all subjects of the Empire together and may enjoy one another's company, provided everyone remembers who is paying for the next round.

# <span id="page-11-0" data-folio="12"></span><span id="page-12-0" data-folio="13"></span><span id="page-13-0" data-folio="14"></span>Our Friends and Allies

Our enlightened Empire extends a hand of friendship to all of good intent, and within its cities can be found halflings, dwarfs, and even elves, all living under the protection of Imperial law and contributing, in their various ways, to the prosperity of our great nation.

Halflings are a common sight as their wandering ancestors recognised the safety of our great city, settled and grew voluminous families. So numerous did they become that, in 1010 **IC**, Emperor Ludwig the Fat granted them their own province, the Mootland. Whether this was intended as a reward for their contributions to Imperial cuisine or, as some less charitable accounts suggest, as a jest, I cannot say. Whatever the original intention, the arrangement has endured rather well. Halflings bake pies, brew ale, run inns and engage in all manner of useful trades, while some serve as archers in the Imperial armies.

They do, however, possess a rather loose appreciation of personal property. A halfling will happily borrow an unattended object without first troubling with the tiresome question of whether the object's owner has consented to the arrangement. Among their own kind this appears to cause little difficulty, perhaps because the distinction between 'mine' and 'yours' is understood to be largely temporary. Among their more proper human neighbours, however, it can cause considerable consternation. I have found that a firm word, a locked cupboard and a clear understanding of whose pie is whose will generally preserve the peace.

Dwarfs, too, have long been valued members of Imperial society, though their friendship is of considerably older standing. The alliance between Sigmar and the dwarfs, sealed when their king presented him with the great hammer Ghal-Maraz, remains one of the foundations upon which our Empire was built. It is therefore only proper that dwarfs should find a welcome here, particularly as their mountain holds continue to stand as a stout line of defence against the enemies that would otherwise trouble our own borders.

Those who leave the holds often find ready employment in the Empire, thanks to their considerable talents and devotion to 'doing a job properly'. Dwarfs are superb smiths, masons, engineers and armourers, and few human craftsmen would not benefit from observing them at work. Many of our finest fortifications were made by dwarf hands, while their expertise in gunpowder and artillery helped Imperial gunnery become renowned and feared wherever its thunderous roars are heard. Their more experimental engineering projects are beyond the comprehension of our best scholars.

<span id="page-14-0" data-folio="15"></span>They are a dour and suspicious people, and their customs can sometimes seem severe to those accustomed to the more flexible habits of Imperial society. Yet a dwarf who calls you friend is a friend indeed, and their loyalty is not lightly abandoned. The same is true of their grievances, which they preserve with a diligence that would be admirable were it applied to less troublesome matters. A dwarf does not forget an insult, a broken promise or a debt left unpaid, and may carry such a grudge well beyond the lifespan of a human. This can make dealings with them somewhat formal, but there is considerable virtue in a people whose word means precisely what they say it means. I have found that courtesy, honesty and the prompt settling of accounts are generally sufficient to maintain good relations.

Then there are the elves, though it would perhaps be more accurate to say that there are elves in the Empire, rather than elves of it. The wood elves of Laurelorn live within Imperial borders but would, I suspect, object rather strongly to being described as Imperial subjects. Their queen is thought to be a mighty sorceress who has the loyalty of her elf subjects, as well as a variety of mysterious spirits of the woods. Few of these elves venture into human lands, and fewer still allow themselves to be properly observed. Their reputation among sophisticated folk is often quite favourable, particularly where music, poetry and other forms of artistry are concerned, while the common citizen is more likely to regard an elf with the same healthy suspicion reserved for wizards and others who seem liable to know rather more than they ought.

They are secretive, furtive, and possessed of customs that make sense only to themselves. It is easy, therefore, to understand their reputation for arrogance. Yet I have encountered several in the course of my studies and, while they are undoubtedly aloof and inscrutable, I have occasionally glimpsed in them something approaching genuine merriment. They seem capable of enjoying themselves enormously, provided they are not required to explain why.

The high elves are rather more visible, maintaining enclaves in some of the Empire's greatest cities, in addition to a few backwaters like Marienburg and l'Anguille, and bringing considerable trade and wealth with them. They are not Imperial subjects either, and don't seem to think our laws apply to them. Their presence is welcomed by merchants and regarded with suspicion by almost everyone else, particularly as it is difficult to tell whether an elf has come to sell silk, purchase timber, or spy.

The aloofness and, dare I say it, arrogance of high elves perhaps explains why elves and dwarfs have yet to truly settle their centuries-old grievance. I have found that courtesy, patience, and a willingness to let an elf have the last word are generally sufficient to maintain good relations — all things our dwarf friends seem reluctant to grant them.

Ogres are much in demand throughout the Empire as bodyguards, mercenaries, and soldiers. Their immense strength and seemingly bottomless appetites are well known, as is their reputation for violence, though their service in Imperial armies suggests they possess rather more discipline than is generally supposed. They are keen travellers and seem remarkably adept at finding some useful place in whatever society they encounter. As for gnomes, I confess there is little to say. They are rarely encountered beyond their secretive moorland homes, and the few accounts I have collected are so contradictory that I suspect most are nonsense.

Beyond our borders, the Empire is surrounded by peoples of varying degrees of usefulness and good sense. To the west lies Bretonnia, a hidebound and somewhat theatrical kingdom, though one which can generally be relied upon to oppose worse sorts of people. The Wasteland, having recently separated itself from Imperial administration, is chiefly concerned with commerce and therefore behaves sensibly whenever there is money to be made. Tilea provides excellent mercenaries, though they do seem to spend an extraordinary amount of time fighting one another. To the east, Kislev remains an intermittent ally, its hardy people understandably occupied with their own affairs.

# <span id="page-15-0" data-folio="16"></span><span id="page-16-0" data-folio="17"></span>On The Empire's Resilience

When Sigmar founded his Empire, he forged the realm through might of arms and triumph of diplomacy, leaving behind a nation so soundly established that it has endured for twenty-five centuries. Our current Emperor, Karl Franz, stands in the rightful succession to that great founder, and occasional periods of disagreement, competing claims, civil wars, and other temporary administrative complications should not distract from this remarkable continuity. No nation survives so long without possessing an excellent foundation.

Even nature herself has helpfully arranged our defences. In the south and west, the mountains rise like the walls of a mighty fortress, while in the north the Sea of Claws provides a most convenient moat. Between them lies the rich and fertile heartland of the Empire, where millions of citizens go about their daily business under the reassuring protection of Imperial law.

Our forests, too, provide valuable natural protection. The mighty woods that divide our towns are extensive enough to discourage casual invasion and supply timber, game and other useful resources. Naturally, one should exercise a little common sense when wandering alone beneath their canopy. There are bandits, wolves, the occasional beastman, and other unfortunate examples of wildlife that have failed to appreciate the benefits of civilisation. From time to time a village is abandoned, a traveller goes missing, or some woodland settlement suffers an unfortunate fire. Such incidents are regrettable, certainly, but hardly evidence of widespread insecurity. Indeed, the fact that Imperial citizens continue to live, farm, trade, and raise families throughout these lands is proof of the protection they enjoy. One cannot expect every tree to have a watchman.

# <span id="page-17-0" data-folio="18"></span><span id="page-18-0" data-folio="19"></span>Threats Without and Within

Our glorious Empire is beset by myriad threats, both beyond its borders and lurking in our wild places. That so many seek to tear down what we have built is proof that our civilisation is the envy of all others.

The notorious Black Fire Pass occasionally disgorges slavering hordes of orcs and goblins intent on sweeping through our lands in a green tide of destruction. Across the Sea of Claws dwell the warriors of the northern wastes, who make war on honest folk in the name of their cruel gods. Sailors tell of black-clad elves seizing whole crews and carrying them away in gargantuan vessels.

Within our borders, tribes of vicious goblins skulk in the deep forests and braying herds of twisted beastmen prey upon isolated villages, which is why sensible settlements are enclosed by stout palisades. Many an explorer or merchant has been set upon from the skies by a hungry griffon or dragged into a mire by the tentacles of a bog octopus. Some even tell of ancient dragons, who dwell deep within mountain lairs, occasionally emerging to menace livestock or roast whole villages alive.

There are, of course, bandits, pirates, and other scoundrels who have yet to find respectable employment, while even our cities harbour cutpurses and ne'er-do-wells. As for the tales of mutants and other horrors lurking beneath our streets, I shall leave such fanciful nonsense to those who enjoy frightening themselves in the dark.

# <span id="page-19-0" data-folio="20"></span>Our Greatest Foe

But there is a yet more sinister and powerful threat, one that sensible people should not discuss frivolously or, preferably, at all. Corruption undoubtedly takes hold in those whose inherent flaws or unfortunate experiences make fertile ground for it, but there are signs for those with the wit to recognise them. The mutant and the blasphemous heretic are undoubtedly created from without as much as from within, touched by an insidious yet inconsistent force that reaches alike into the wild places and the centres of civilisation.

Great care should therefore be taken even when mildly touching upon such subjects. Many fervent followers of our gods would put an end to anyone even discussing such matters, apparently on the principle that corruption enters the mouth of those who speak of it. Horrifyingly, they may have the right of it. Many who investigate dark mysteries are consumed by them, one way or another. I have always considered that the wise man must balance curiosity with a broader awareness, and know when a subject is best not investigated further.

However they come about, these darker forces appeal to our baser instincts, just as Sigmar appeals to our bravery, Shallya to our mercy, and Verena to our reason. This corruption can take many routes into one's soul. Disease and pestilence can cause such great suffering in a person or their kin that they will perform acts of deranged appeasement as though to deflect such an awful fate.

Violence and the spilling of blood can become an insatiable drive. Hiding from the trials of life in obsessive overindulgence of their heart's desires traps others. And then there's the scholar's peril, thirst for knowledge taking one unknowingly into the dark from which there is no return.

Though the signs of these dark powers may be plain enough among our more monstrous enemies, I fear there are more souls lost to damnation living quietly among us than any sensible person would care to contemplate. Fortunately, the truly depraved are generally quite easy to identify once they have acquired horns, tentacles, or some other conspicuous indication of poor character. The more troublesome cases are those in which no such outward sign presents itself.

Not all monsters look monstrous. Indeed, some appear perfectly respectable, attending temple, conducting honest business, and behaving with every appearance of decency, until some unfortunate circumstance causes the mask to slip and the horror beneath to reveal itself.

Worse still are the secretive cults that shelter such people, hiding themselves while spreading poisonous doctrines behind respectable doors. They may offer sanctuary to mutants, outlawed witches, and practitioners of forbidden arts, drawing their membership from every level of society. Scholar and labourer, noble and beggar: corruption, it seems, is distressingly democratic.

The most dangerous of these deviants scheme to recruit and place agents where they may do the greatest harm, insinuating themselves into households, guilds, courts and even military garrisons. Their leaders promise followers grand destinies and glorious transformation, though I confess that the promised future generally sounds rather less glorious once one learns that it involves the destruction of everything presently standing.

The prudent citizen, and especially gullible, impressionable youths, should therefore be wary of secret societies, mysterious midnight gatherings and anyone who insists that he possesses knowledge which the rest of us are not yet ready to understand. There is rarely anything worth knowing at the end of such a conversation.

# <span id="page-20-0" data-folio="21"></span>A Civilisation Worth Saving

Despite the many and varied threats that seek to tear down the grand undertaking that is the Empire, we have endured for centuries, and will do so for centuries more. Our grand cities are not just bastions against the onslaught of our enemies, but the hubs of civilisation where opportunity abounds for industrious and ingenious souls.

The indomitable spirit of commerce leads canny merchants to seek ever greater avenues of trade, sponsoring expeditions to the darkest corners of the world in search of rare and valuable resources that the people of Altdorf will pay handsome coin for, once they have been liberated from whatever unfortunate creatures foolishly consider them their own. Nobles, merchants and guilds seeking advantage over one another create myriad opportunities for ambitious individuals. Even the less salubrious aspects of city life can lead one to great wealth and sometimes even respectability. Many prominent Imperial citizens have roots in activities that polite society knows are best left uninterrogated, lest they undermine the good those individuals have done with their coin since becoming respectable. Meanwhile, the institutions of law and order offer gainful employment to investigators and civic-minded folk willing to root out criminals and foolish conspiracies that the diligent watch lacks the time to address. I might also humbly recommend my own craft, the interrogative scribblings of the scholar, which has provided sufficient coin and diversion to be considered an adequate life's calling.

Beyond the walls of our cities and towns, the forests and hills harbour ancient ruins awaiting adventurous sorts to seek them out, uncover their mysteries, and return laden with treasures. That such places are sometimes occupied by goblins, beastmen or other unfortunate wildlife merely offers the added satisfaction of making the land safer by eradicating such creatures. In fact, the fortified villages and towns of the Empire are run, in the main, by practical-minded burghers who will happily pay good coin for those of a martial bent to deal with any local threats to life and limb. The noble members of our knightly orders dedicate their lives to such an honourable pursuit.

And so, to our restless young people who want to contribute to the wellbeing of this Empire we all love, I say this is the way to make your mark. There's no glory to be had in bad poetry, politics and the pox. Put your efforts into endeavours that will bring profit to you and improvement to our land.

Many a tale has been told around roaring hearths of bands of ambitious, hardy sorts that have struck out from the Empire's safe havens into the trackless wilds in search of glory and riches. True, such tales invariably include encounters with slavering greenskins, lurking horrors, and servants of darker forces, but they are also rife with moments of heroism, indomitable courage, and, importantly, usually end with the surviving members returning home with armfuls of gold, gems, and magical artefacts. There are those who, hearing such tales in the tavern, are prone to scoff at their fanciful nature but let me tell you, from someone who has travelled the length and breadth of this fine Empire of ours, there is far more awaiting the adventurous soul out there than in any drinking den of empty-headed firebrands.

Before you cry that I offer medicine I would not take myself, consider that it was at my urging that Stefan has but recently returned from the thrice-cursed county of Sylvania, with bountiful riches of folklore and music. Given his outburst I think I'll be sending him on another quest shortly, especially as Frau Weirde mentioned to me that we could get a handful of silver a week for a respectable room in Altdorf these days.
