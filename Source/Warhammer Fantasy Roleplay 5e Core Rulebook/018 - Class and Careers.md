# <span id="page-42-0" data-folio="43"></span>• **CLASS AND CAREERS** •

*'Well, I play a classless knave who would sell his own grandmother for a promotion and, when called upon regarding his shortcomings, blames those who do the actual work.'*

— Dietmar Kesselborn, Playwright and Actor

Your Career is your Character's job when they are not off adventuring. It describes their training, social class, and an indication of their future prospects.

## **CLASSES**

Each Career belongs to a Class, such as Riverfolk or Warriors. A list of each class Class and its associated Careers is found in **Chapter 2: Character** on page 36. Classes impact character development, provide different starting Trappings during Character Creation (see page 39), and influence how you can change Career (see *Change Career* on page 197).

## **CAREERS**

The summary and illustration at the top of the eight Careers in your chosen Class should give you a good idea of which is the one for you. The Careers can be considered to be as broad as possible, so you are encouraged to interpret them imaginatively as part of your Character concept.

## **Advancing Through Your Career**

You can move to the next level in your Career by improving your abilities and collecting the trappings it requires. Tick one of the boxes between the levels of your Career on you Character Sheet each time you:

- Advance a Career Skill or Characteristic
- Gain a Talent from your Career
- Acquire a Trapping from the next level of your Career

When you have filled the ten boxes, you can take the Advance Career Endeavour on page 196 to move to the next Level.

You can also opt to change to a new Career entirely by paying XP and working with the GM to determine if and how you may do so. See the *Change Career* Endeavour on page 197 for more.

## **Anatomy of a Career**

To understand your Career, there are six main things you need to pay attention to: Career Levels, Characteristics, Skills, Talents, Trappings, and Status.

## **1. Career Levels**

Every Career has four levels, each progressively better than the last. When choosing a Career for a new Character, you always start at the first level of the Career — so, if you were a Soldier, you would start as a Recruit.

## **2. Characteristics**

Every Career has an *Advance Scheme* governing its Characteristic Advances. The Advance Scheme shows your character's ten Characteristics, three marked with h, one marked with on a green background, one marked with a on silver, and the last marked on gold.

The three marked h are Characteristics you can Advance in the Career's first level. When you enter a Career's second level, the Characteristic also becomes available, and so on. You can Advance any Characteristic at or below your current career level. See page 191 for how to spend XP.

## <span id="page-43-0" data-folio="44"></span>**3. Skills**

You can Advance all the Skills listed for your current Career level and lower. So, if you were a Recruit (Soldier level 1), you could only access the Skills listed under Recruit (level 1). But, if you were a Sergeant (level 3), you could access all the Skills for Sergeant (level 3), Soldier (level 2), and Recruit (level 1).

**Note:** One of the Skills in your Career's first level will be marked in ***bold italics***. This is the Skill you Test when earning money with the *Income* Endeavour during Downtime (see page 199).

## **NON-CAREER ADVANCES**

You can purchase Advances for Characteristics and Basic Skills that are not in your Career for double the cost listed in the **Characteristic and Skill Advance XP Costs** table (page 191). Normally, you can only acquire non-Career Advanced Skills and Talents through the *Training* and *Unusual Learning* Endeavours in **Chapter 6: Between Adventures**.

## **4. Talents**

You may purchase Talents listed in your current Career level, plus any Talents listed in your Career's lower levels. So a Sergeant (Soldier level 3) could purchase Talents available to: Recruits (level 1), Soldiers (level 2), and Sergeants.

## **5. Trappings**

These are the Trappings typical of a Character at each Career Level. When creating a Character, you receive all of the Trappings listed at Career Level 1. So a Recruit would begin play with the Trappings of the Warrior Class, as well as a Leather Breastplate, Uniform, and a Weapon of their choice. You will have to acquire the Trappings of higher Career Levels by yourself.

## **6. Status**

This shows the Status of your current Career Level. As long as you have at least one Trapping from your current Career level, you can use this Status. If you don't, or if you have otherwise disgraced yourself, your Status is lowered (see page 141).

Advisers provide counsel to those they serve. Well-versed in the social conditions of their employer's domain, they are privy to confidential and sensitive information, or possess keen political insights. While many Advisers are born into their positions, others actively seek patronage as a path to wealth and influence, looking for nobles, successful merchants, crime lords, and other powerful individuals to offer service to. In turn, these patrons look for those they can trust to help them prosper.

## <span id="page-45-0" data-folio="46"></span>**AGITATOR**

**Burgher Class:** Dwarf, Halfling, Human

*Charismatic and articulate, you champion your cause and try to change the world.*

#### *'ALTDORF FOR ALTDORFERS! MIDDENLANDERS OUT!'*

— Pamphlet, Street of a Hundred Taverns, Altdorf

*'Listen, if you're looking to root out agents of the Spinner of Fate, follow the clamour in the streets. They can't resist it. They'll surface, sooner or later.'*

— Cleric-captain Wulfscheid, Knight of the Fiery Heart

Agitators champion political and religious causes using print, protest, and public speaking. They muster the support of the downtrodden, hoping the masses can force change. Whether they call for the radical overthrow of the ruling classes or reactionary support for the status quo, they stir up discontent against those they blame for the world's ills. The most successful Agitators have destabilised the rule of nobles, towns, and even entire provinces.

Pamphleteers nail signs to billboards or distribute them in market squares, though often those they seek to reach cannot read. Religious Agitators can make a good living as street preachers, receiving donations from pious devotees and attracting flagellants

and zealots as followers. Agitators who survive long enough to become Demagogues are often supported by powerful, hidden allies pursuing change for their own ends. Most Agitators are sincere in their beliefs, though some are cynics and opportunists who exploit discontent for personal gain.

Agitators often move around, in search of larger crowds or fleeing the authorities. They may lead motley bands of the restless and disaffected, exhorting them to fight for a greater cause. Some Agitators travel from village to village, drumming up support, but most are found in towns and cities where it is easier to sway crowds.

As an Agitator, you can sway crowds in your favour and help your party negotiate the back and forth of city life, not to mention pick (and hopefully win) an argument with almost anyone.

## <span id="page-46-0" data-folio="47"></span>**APOTHECARY**

**Academic Class:** Dwarf, Halfling, High Elf, Human

*You use your knowledge of chemistry and concoction to mix and trade all manner of medicines and remedies.*

*'Fractured femur? I'll need a hare's gall bladder, sour milk, powdered limestone, nettle essence, and three days to prepare it. Drink daily at dawn and the bone should knit back together, but don't breathe on anyone you want to impress.'*

— Elvyra Jung, Apothecary

*'Manling remedies? As shoddy as their stonework! and just as likely to kill you! Asked for a tonic to cure this toothache. Had the flux for a week and my tooth is still throbbing like an apprentice's thumb!'*

— Thorica Norrasdottir, Dwarf Weaponsmith

Apothecaries specialise in preparing pills, draughts, and salves, substances created to affect the body in some way. While most of these preparations possess medicinal qualities, a skilled apothecary can also craft stimulants, poisons, and other compounds with dubious effects.

An Apothecary's workshop is filled with a dazzling array of bubbling alembics, overflowing crucibles, mortars and pestles, and other strange paraphernalia. Apothecaries may embark on expeditions to find exotic ingredients, to research new concoctions, or recover lost pharmacopoeia and forgotten formulae. They are valued travelling companions, not least for their skills as healers. Most physicians' guilds forbid Apothecaries from practising medicine in towns and cities, so an itinerant life often proves more lucrative.

Playing an Apothecary lets you heal or harm with equal skill. You can become an invaluable compatriot or a subtle danger to your enemies, thanks to your skill with poisons. You might grow rich selling your services on the road, find a noble patron who values your talents for good or ill, or dedicate yourself to discovering new ingredients and inventing new recipes.

## <span id="page-47-0" data-folio="48"></span>**ARTISAN**

**Burgher Class:** Dwarf, Halfling, High Elf, Human, Wood Elf

*A skilled craftsperson, you take pride in your work, creating products for sale and commission.*

*'This hatter from Wolfenburg said he was a guilder in good standing. We ditched him after a week, I've never seen shoddier work. I don't know what passes for a stiff brim in Ostland, but our guild wouldn't put that on a mule.'* — Gunter Mühlenau, Middenheim Milliners' Guild

Artisans make goods, ranging from everyday necessities sold by bakers and chandlers, to weapons and riverboats crafted by smiths and shipwrights. In the Empire's larger towns and cities, craft guilds protect Artisans by keeping prices fair and dealing with unscrupulous competitors. They enforce strict quality standards, and those who fail to meet them are forbidden to trade locally.

Every Artisan must serve an apprenticeship to learn their craft before earning the right to sell their services. Many become Journeymen, working for a Master Artisan, travelling as itinerant craftsmen, or attaching themselves to the military to maintain and repair equipment. As their skill and reputation grow, Artisans rise within their guild, eventually acquiring the influence and political power of a Guildmaster.

An Artisan's skills are valuable almost everywhere, so playing this career could prove lucrative. You will be able to make contact with local guildsmen and, providing you can prove your skill, quickly acquire valuable connections. In time, you may create a true masterpiece of your craft and earn the acclaim of important patrons.

## <span id="page-48-0" data-folio="49"></span>**ARTIST**

**Courtier Class:** Dwarf, Halfling, High Elf, Human, Wood Elf

*You possess an artistic gift, and strive to create works that uplift the soul… or at least pay the bills.*

*'My lord, Master Erich's latest works twist reality into unnatural and grotesque shapes. I fear his hand is guided by darker inspirations than a good servant of Sigmar might tolerate. I report this solely for the safety of the Empire, not from personal envy or my desire to attract your patronage.'*

— Eckhart Silbermann, Painter of Averheim

Artists use their talents for painting, sculpting, writing, and similar pursuits to create works of fine art. Most begin as Apprentices to experienced Master Artists, though some are prodigies whose talent emerges untrained. The finest attract wealthy patrons, establish studios of their own, found new artistic movements, and earn considerable acclaim. Art is always in demand across the Old World, particularly among the religious cults and ruling classes.

Sadly, most Artists spend their lives struggling to prove the value of their work to a society that rarely appreciates them. Some make ends meet by satirising nobles and politicians in cartoons for the Altdorf broadsheets, sketching suspects for watch captains, or copying the work of more celebrated Artists. Others flatter the powerful, producing art of little merit to satisfy their patrons.

Artists of all stripes find themselves travelling abroad in search of patronage and recognition. Even established or privately wealthy Artists take to the road in search of new sights to inspire them. Playing an Artist is an opportunity to explore the world with fresh eyes, lend an air of (apparent) respectability to the party, and apply your creative talents in surprisingly versatile ways.

## <span id="page-49-0" data-folio="50"></span>**BAILIFF**

**Peasant Class:** Dwarf, Halfling, Human

*You are trusted by a local ruler to represent the law and administer their lands with a firm hand.*

*'Aye, it's been a poor harvest, but dues are dues. I'll take half now and half on Marktag. How's that? It's the best I can offer, can't say fairer than that.'*

— Lena Sauer, Bailiff

*'The reeve is after a chimney tax now! A crown per chimney, payable every month! Fetch the lump hammer, Ilse, the smoke can find its own way out.'*

— Wilbur Bauer, Farmer (deceased)

Nobles entrust Bailiffs to represent their authority and collect dues from their lands. Some are respected and upstanding, treating their lord's tenants with fairness and a sense of justice. Others are bullies, enforcing their lord's rights over property and possessions with threats and violence. The most ruthless become minor despots in their own right, laying down the law and extracting taxes without pity.

Most Bailiffs begin as Tax Collectors, but may rise to become Reeves with broader authority to enforce their lord's law. They keep order, maintain the borders of the estate, and resolve disputes with neighbouring lands. The most senior become Magistrates, arbitrating disputes and prosecuting offences against their liege lord.

Bailiffs enjoy a degree of autonomy, granting them ample opportunity to turn their attention to private matters. Their broad authority also means they are often sent by their employers to resolve problems beyond the boundaries of the estate.

Playing a Bailiff lets you wield your lord's authority and act in their name. You can intimidate, hold your own in a fight, and move comfortably between legal and illegal methods to get things done.

## <span id="page-51-0" data-folio="52"></span>**BOATMAN**

**Riverfolk Class:** Dwarf, Halfling, High Elf, Human

*You ply the dangerous waters of the Old World, transporting people, goods, and the latest news.*

*'The Stir is no gentle river, lads. Under the placid surface the current twists, battering barges like driftwood. Mind your oars, trim your sails, and keep your wits. Should a man fall overboard downstream of Wurtbad they'll make a fine morsel for the stirpike that haunt those waters.'*

— Jakob 'Rivertooth' Brücken, Veteran Boatman of the Stir

*'Get a move on, you lazy oafs! We've got a hold full of Ravolan Frizzante due for Altdorf and I won't have you take so long casting off it turns to vinegar.'*

— Hector Weber, Bargeswain

Boats and barges travel the Empire's myriad waterways, carrying goods from the remotest corners of the provinces to towns and cities. These shallow-drafted vessels can travel much further upriver than larger ships, while a vast network of canals extends their reach.

On board, experience dictates your role. A Boathand is the dogsbody, still learning the ropes. Boatmen crew merchant barges carrying cargo between markets, either working for themselves or for a larger merchant company.

Many also work as ferrymen or rivertaxi operators, carrying passengers across rivers and between settlements. Barge Masters are expert river sailors and have an exhaustive knowledge of their waterways.

Boatmen often find themselves caught up in adventures, whether they like it or not. Their vessels allow them to investigate events along the rivers, and even when their travels take them onto dry land, they can leave a trusted Boathand to keep business running.

As a Boatman, you are strong, tough, and accustomed to handling yourself in rough weather and rougher dockside taverns. Eventually, you can also provide your party with a mobile base of operations to travel the Empire.
