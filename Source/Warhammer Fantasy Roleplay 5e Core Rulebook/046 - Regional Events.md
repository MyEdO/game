# <span id="page-191-0" data-folio="192"></span>**REGIONAL EVENTS**

The GM should roll once on the Regional Event table to determine what's going on in the local area during the Characters' downtime. If there is some important event, local festival or misfortune, or a prominent piece of gossip doing the rounds that will be important to a future adventure, the GM can substitute that instead of rolling.

#### **01–02: Grand Procession**

Crowds flock to the roadside to watch as a great procession of the faithful, dedicated to a god of the Empire chosen by the GM, winds its way through the region. Warrior priests, wild figures in animal skins, chanting clergy, or solemn mourners accompany the procession, depending on the cult. Characters devoted to the deity being honoured are inspired by the spectacle, and may choose to make a single Test with Advantage during the next adventure.

#### **03–04: Unusual Mercenaries**

One or more rare mercenaries turn up in a nearby settlement looking for work: a Tilean Duellist of great repute, the so-called Birdmen of Catrazza, unemployed ogres under the command of a halfling captain, or other unlikely bands. The mercenaries will gladly train any Character in any martial Skills or Talents, at a 20% reduction in costs, should the Characters undertake a *Training* or *Unusual Learning* Endeavour.

#### **05–07: Auguries of the West Wind**

The surrounding area suffers from a severe drought. In desperation, the local lord sends for help from the Colleges of Magic in Altdorf, who dispatch a wizard with a mighty Celestial Hurricanum. As the esoteric device scours the local landscape with wind and rain, it not only terrifies the locals, but also draws the Blue Wind of *Azyr*. All Casting and Channelling Tests involving *Azyr* during the next adventure gain +1 SL.

#### **08–12: Teacher, Teacher!**

A travelling tutor sets up shop in the local square, offering to teach any and all in exchange for coin. The GM chooses an Advanced Skill. This Downtime, any Character may use the *Training* Endeavour to learn that skill, without having to use *Consult an Expert* to find a teacher.

#### **13–16: The Doktor is In**

A travelling barber surgeon arrives in the region, driving a wagon packed with medical supplies and a reclining leather chair only lightly stained with blood. Scarce and Rare Herbs and Draughts (page 314) have their Availability improved by one step during this Downtime. They will also happily perform any amputations, tooth extractions, or haircuts for free as a demonstration to draw in coin-laden crowds.

#### **17–21: Artisan Caravan**

A caravan of dwarfen artisans stops off on its journey between holds, willing to trade superior crafting materials. All Tests taken as part of a *Crafting* Endeavour during this Downtime benefit from +2 SL.

#### **22–26: Flowing Profit**

Disruption on major roads due to brigands, beastmen, or whatever threatens the locale drives trade to the waterways. All Riverfolk make an additional 50% from any *Income* Endeavour pursued during this Downtime.

#### **27–30: Opportunity Saunters Through**

Passing soldiers, well-heeled merchants, or vacationing nobles pass through the area, and those willing to curry favour reap the benefits. Burgher and Peasant Class Characters make an additional 50% from any *Income* Endeavour pursued during this Downtime.

#### **31–35: Festivities**

A celebration is called! The GM decides the nature of the event. Possibilities include a local wedding, a feast of Rhya in thanks for a bountiful harvest, or a public execution! With so many people around, any Gossip Tests made during this downtime benefit from Advantage, while Artists and Entertainers double their money from the *Income* Endeavour.

#### **36–39: Marvellous Miracle Cures**

A travelling potion salesman arrives in the region, driving a gaudy wagon bedecked with racks of mysterious liquids in vials, jars, and earthen jugs. Scarce and Rare Herbs and Draughts (page 314) have their Availability improved by one step if bought from the travelling merchant during this Downtime. For each item bought this way, the GM rolls a d10: on a 1 or 2, it is actually a Poison (page 313) of the GM's choosing.

#### <span id="page-192-0" data-folio="193"></span>**40–42: Levy**

A local lord levies warriors to form a regiment in his name. Maybe they are looking to contribute to a wider war effort, assemble a private army for their own purposes, or just keep up appearances through sponsoring a temporary military exercise. Characters from the Warrior Class gain an additional 50% from any *Income* Endeavours as they assist in training or recruiting villagers. Warriors who undertake the *Income* Endeavour count as having taken the *Combat Training* Endeavour.

#### **43–45: Travelling Players**

A colourful troupe of travelling players arrives, erecting a makeshift stage in the market square and performing tales of heroic deeds, tragic romances, and biting satires of local worthies. The local worthies swiftly have them arrested and ban all public gatherings as seditious. *Consult an Expert*, *Foment Dissent*, and *The Latest News* each require 1 additional Endeavour this Downtime as people become fearful of speaking openly.

#### **46–52: Inclement Weather**

Particularly bad weather arrives. For the next adventure, all social Skill Tests suffer a penalty of -1 SL (everyone is in a foul mood due to the rain), and food prices increase by 50% due to spoiling of stores.

#### **53–55: Religious Mania**

A brief but intense period of religious mania breaks out, perhaps due to unexplained celestial phenomena, the preaching of a radical prophet, or a beast born with the sign of one of the gods. Any Pray Tests made during this Downtime benefit from Advantage.

#### **56–59: Power Vacuum**

A prominent local leader — such as a guildmaster, village elder, or watch captain — is suddenly replaced due to illness, scandal, or looking askance at a passing witch hunter. The local political landscape is a powderkeg as factions vie for power in the wake of the change. *Foment Dissent* only requires 1 Endeavour, rather than 2, this Downtime.

#### **60–64: New Moon**

The nights are particularly dark, providing ideal cover for those who work in the shadows. Rogue Characters undertaking the *Income* Endeavour this Downtime increase the money they earn by 50%.

#### **65–68: False Silver**

To the horror of the merchants, a run of counterfeit coins plagues the locale. Characters who undertake the *Banking* Endeavour will lose 20% of money banked, and those undertaking the *Income* Endeavour similarly reduce any money earned by 20%.

#### **69–73: Tax Collector**

An unexpected visit by the tax collector (with several soldiers in tow) sweeps through the settlement. Those with legal employment earn 50% less from any *Income* Endeavours due to harsh taxes, while Rogues and others who operate outside the law earn double the usual amount as opportunities for smuggling and dirty dealing abound.

#### **74–81: Crime Crackdown**

The Watch is cracking down hard. Rogues can get no funds from the *Income* Endeavour, and if Rogues undertake a *Banking*  Endeavour, they may only select a high-risk deposit, as legitimate banking houses won't handle dirty money right now.

#### **82–88: Glorious Weather**

Particularly sunny weather arrives. For the next adventure, all social Skill Tests suffer a penalty of -1 SL (everyone is in a foul mood due to the heat). Dry roads make trade easier, however, increasing the availability of all non-Exotic goods by one step.

#### **89–91: Local Crop Failure**

Food becomes exceedingly scarce and many folk suffer terribly. Characters from the Peasant Class cannot undertake the *Income*  Endeavour, and food prices are doubled in the area for the duration of the next adventure.

#### **92–94: Malicious Malady**

The Washers Guild are hard at work as the Bloody Flux sweeps through town. Each Character must pass an **Easy (+4 SL) Endurance** Test or contract the Bloody Flux (page 180) on a failure.

#### **95–97: Riots**

Due to a sudden tax increase, public scandal, or the ravings of a deranged prophet, the common folk are furious at the high and mighty! Characters of the Courtier Class cannot take the *Income* Endeavour as, no matter how many bodyguards they hire, it isn't even vaguely safe for them to be abroad on business. Players attempting a *Foment Dissent* Endeavour benefit from Advantage on any related Tests.

#### **98–00: Under Suspicion**

The party's irregular movements or sudden wealth has drawn suspicion. The Characters become acquainted with the local magistrate, and must forfeit one Endeavour either laying low or proving their innocence. Characters in the Rogue class cannot use the *Income* Endeavour until after their next adventure.
